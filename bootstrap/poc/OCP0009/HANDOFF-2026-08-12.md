## 2. WHAT LEXREC ACTUALLY IS, in the three places it matters

**The two recursors.** `rec₁` is `aIHT` — amrec's IH VERBATIM
(`rec1T = aIHT` is a definition, not an analogy). `rec₂` is new:
`(y : A) → μ₁ y ≤ μ₁ x → μ₂ y < μ₂ x → P y`, two descents.

⚠ **Neither combinator derives from the other, and there is a hard
reason:** lex order on ℕ² has order type ω², and ω² does not embed in ω,
so no single ℕ-valued measure reflects it. `amrec` is exactly "recursion
on one ℕ measure" and provably cannot do lexrec's job. What they share is
the SUBSTRATE — `LibWk`'s 27 lemmas, `LibRec`'s IH types,
`⊢strong-base'`/`⊢strong-step` — not the recursor.

**The three layers.** Inner `natrec` on n₂ twice ((0,0)/(0,S) under the
outer zero branch, (S,0)/(S,S) under the outer step); outer `natrec` on n₁
with motive `lexMot`, whose body is `Π Nat …` — n₂ quantified INSIDE,
which is exactly "when μ₁ drops, μ₂ is unconstrained"; then `Asm` applied
to ITSELF at `Δ ▹ A`, so both bounds are the measure families and both
order obligations are reflexivity.

**The four branches.** (0,0) both recursors vacuous; (0,S) rec₁ vacuous,
rec₂ real; (S,0) rec₁ real with the n₂ RESET, rec₂ vacuous; (S,S) both
real. The reset — instantiating the outer IH's `Π Nat` at μ₂ y and
discharging its μ₂ obligation by `⊢le-refl` — is the lexicographic move.

## 3. ⭐ LEXREC IS WRAPPED UP.  NEXT IS AMREC + gcd.

### Why lexrec stops here — the D11 finding

⚠ **No function has been found that REQUIRES `⊢lexrec`, and there is a
mechanism for why** (`WF-LIBRARY.md` D11). Its own auxiliary is
`natrec lexZ lexS n` with `lexZ = lam (natrec …)` — an outer recursion
returning a FUNCTION — which is *literally* `SpikeAckT`'s construction,
plus order certificates. It is a derived term, so it cannot add
definitional power; the certificates are overhead wherever a structural
form exists. Ackermann both ways: **26 lines / 0.61 s** without it against
**~100 lines / 8.8 s** through it.

⚠⚠ **DO NOT READ THAT AS A MARK AGAINST THE WF AXIS.** The axis and the
libraries are different things. The libraries add no expressive power —
that is not what they are for — they exist so WF recursion is SIMPLER TO
WRITE. The axis is justified by `div` and `gcd`, which are non-structural
and genuinely need a measure, and that is `⊢amrec`'s job.

⇒ lexrec's claim is **parity and ergonomics** (Agda's own checker, Coq's
`lexprod`, Isabelle's `fun`, ACL2 all have lexicographic termination
first-class). It is derived, callable, carrier-generic, documented and
green. **That is enough. Do not spend more on it** — in particular D7 for
lexrec is NOT worth building until a caller needs to reason about its
reduction, and its unfolding is a nested `natrec`, so it is expensive.

### ⭐ THE NEXT TASK — gcd through `⊢amrecΠ`

The use site that actually earns the axis, and `WF-LIBRARY.md`'s own
stated requirement for the next one: *"a recursion whose termination is
NOT free, at a carrier that is NOT ℕ… a pair carrier with a measure that
is a real computation rather than a projection — e.g. `μ (a , b) = a + b`."*

Subtractive Euclid is exactly that:

```
gcd (a , 0) = a
gcd (0 , b) = b
gcd (a , b) = gcd (a ∸ b , b)   if a > b     -- μ = a+b drops, b>0
gcd (a , b) = gcd (a , b ∸ a)   if b > a     -- μ = a+b drops, a>0
```

* carrier `Σ' Nat Nat` — `NbEPDirDBLibPair` already ships it;
* measure `a + b` — a REAL computation, not `fst`;
* **⚠ `⊢amrec`, not `⊢lexrec`** — one ℕ measure suffices, and `a+b` is why.

⚠ **`⊢gcd-descend` IS NOT THIS.** It is `⊢div-descend` renamed, and
`SpikePairT`'s header already records that it "certifies a recursion that
was not gcd" — the one-sided `gcd (suc m) (suc k) = gcd (m ∸ k) (suc k)`
gives `gcd 3 5 = 5`. Real gcd needs BOTH branches, hence the comparison.

⚠ **What makes it work, and why it was deferred before:** the descent
needs monotonicity of `+` under `≤` and its strict form — `SpikePairT`'s
header calls that "a real arithmetic development". That development IS the
task; budget it. `⊢monus-le` and `monusTm` (`…ExamplesDiv`) are the
starting point, and a comparison (`a > b`) has to be built too, since the
kernel has no coproduct — expect it to come out as a `natrec` on `a ∸ b`.

## 4. TRAPS

* ⭐ **PROBE, DO NOT GUESS.** Every derivation failure across both
  sessions was bookkeeping — a miscounted weakening, a misplaced cast, a
  `sym` the wrong way round — never a design problem. Put `⊢nzero` where
  the derivation goes; the `UnequalTerms` error prints the goal in full.
  And probe **one premise at a time**.
* ★ **`dmX`'s shape is DEPTH-INDEPENDENT.** Across all four branches it is
  `⊢wk³ (⊢wkᶠ^d dm)` — always exactly THREE ordinary weakenings above the
  carrier slot (`le`, `lt`, the `y` binder), with `⊢wkᶠ` for everything
  below. Reach for that rule; do not count from the top.
* ⚠ **Motives must be spelled IDENTICALLY across branch modules.** `mot`
  in `ZZ`/`ZS`, `omot`/`imot` in `SZ`/`SS`. The assembly needs them to be
  the same terms, not merely equal ones.
* ⚠ **Timing needs a COLD interface.** A second `check.sh` run reports
  only load time — measured 2.80 s against 8.73 s cold. `touch` does NOT
  invalidate it; `find _build -name '*<Module>*.agdai' -delete`.
* ⚠ **RSS varies ±12% run to run.** One sample each; a <15% difference
  between modules is not evidence.
* ⚠ **`w` vs `wᶠ`, and `⊢wk` vs `⊢wkᶠ`.** `wᶠ` inserts a slot BELOW a
  family's own variable. **The count is wrong more often than the kind.**

  `wᶠ-sub` each need a pointwise bridge. Budget one whenever a lemma
  moves a family under `extR`.

  obligation. That is deliberate.
