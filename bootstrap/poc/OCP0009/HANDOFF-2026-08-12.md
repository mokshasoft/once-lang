## 2. WHAT LEXREC ACTUALLY IS, in the three places it matters

**The two recursors.** `rec₁` is `aIHT` — amrec's IH VERBATIM
(`rec1T = aIHT` is a definition, not an analogy). `rec₂` is new:
`(y : A) → μ₁ y ≤ μ₁ x → μ₂ y < μ₂ x → P y`, two descents.

⚠ **Neither combinator derives from the other, and there is a hard
reason:** lex order on ℕ² has order type ω², and ω² does not embed in ω,
so no single ℕ-valued measure reflects it. `amrec` is exactly "recursion
on one ℕ measure" and provably cannot do lexrec's job. What they share is
the SUBSTRATE — `LibWk`'s 27 lemmas, `LibRec`'s IH types,
`⊢strong-base'`/`⊢strong-step` — not the recursor.

**The three layers.** Inner `natrec` on n₂ twice ((0,0)/(0,S) under the
outer zero branch, (S,0)/(S,S) under the outer step); outer `natrec` on n₁
with motive `lexMot`, whose body is `Π Nat …` — n₂ quantified INSIDE,
which is exactly "when μ₁ drops, μ₂ is unconstrained"; then `Asm` applied
to ITSELF at `Δ ▹ A`, so both bounds are the measure families and both
order obligations are reflexivity.

**The four branches.** (0,0) both recursors vacuous; (0,S) rec₁ vacuous,
rec₂ real; (S,0) rec₁ real with the n₂ RESET, rec₂ vacuous; (S,S) both
real. The reset — instantiating the outer IH's `Π Nat` at μ₂ y and
discharging its μ₂ obligation by `⊢le-refl` — is the lexicographic move.

## 3. ⭐ WHAT TO DO NEXT

1. **D7 — lexrec ships no COMPUTATION RULE.** `LibAmrec` has `amrec-β`,
   `amrec-unfold-z/-s` and `AmTΠ◇.amrec-unfold`; lexrec has nothing. By
   WF-LIBRARY D7 that means every caller who wants to know their function
   COMPUTES must unfold the combinator's internals by hand — and lexrec's
   unfolding is a NESTED natrec, so it is worse here than it was for
   amrec. ★ This is the largest remaining gap in the interface.
2. **Ackermann (#9)** — the use site that needs BOTH recursors.
   `SpikeLexUse` uses `rec₂` only. Ackermann's step splits on both
   components and makes three recursive calls (`ack(m,1)` and the outer
   `ack(m,·)` through rec₁, the inner `ack(m+1,n)` through rec₂), so it
   needs the motive to abstract BOTH bounds — two nested `natrec`s inside
   the step, each in the `SpikeLexUse.fMot` style.
3. **CONSOLIDATE.** Six spike modules become `NbEPDirDBLibLexrec` (plus
   per-branch modules if the traversal cost demands it — the peak is
   1.74 GB against a 5.5 GB cap, so one module may well fit). Move
   `sub-wTy`, `wᶠ-sub` into `LibWk` where they belong; they are generic
   substitution metatheory and are currently local to `SpikeLexT`.
4. **Retire the legacy modules** — `AmrecC`, `AmrecT`, `LexC`, `LexCMot`,
   the `LexC*` branches, option B's `Lex*`. That also kills the inverted
   dependency (`AmrecC` imports `…ExamplesLexC`).
5. **`#11`** — genericity — was the other original motivation and is
   unblocked.

### 📌 An open question worth one spike, not a detour

Structurally lexrec IS amrec's pattern nested: doubly-bounded `auxB` with
nested `natrec` against amrec's singly-bounded `aAuxB` with one. So
"could `⊢lexrecΠ` be built by calling `⊢amrecΠ` twice?" is not silly. The
obstacle is that the inner recursion must run over the fibre
`{y | μ₁ y ≡ μ₁ x}`, which needs a `Σ'` carrier carrying the equality.
Untested. It would not change the interface, only the derivation.

## 4. TRAPS

* ⭐ **PROBE, DO NOT GUESS.** Every derivation failure across both
  sessions was bookkeeping — a miscounted weakening, a misplaced cast, a
  `sym` the wrong way round — never a design problem. Put `⊢nzero` where
  the derivation goes; the `UnequalTerms` error prints the goal in full.
  And probe **one premise at a time**.
* ★ **`dmX`'s shape is DEPTH-INDEPENDENT.** Across all four branches it is
  `⊢wk³ (⊢wkᶠ^d dm)` — always exactly THREE ordinary weakenings above the
  carrier slot (`le`, `lt`, the `y` binder), with `⊢wkᶠ` for everything
  below. Reach for that rule; do not count from the top.
* ⚠ **Motives must be spelled IDENTICALLY across branch modules.** `mot`
  in `ZZ`/`ZS`, `omot`/`imot` in `SZ`/`SS`. The assembly needs them to be
  the same terms, not merely equal ones.
* ⚠ **Timing needs a COLD interface.** A second `check.sh` run reports
  only load time — measured 2.80 s against 8.73 s cold. `touch` does NOT
  invalidate it; `find _build -name '*<Module>*.agdai' -delete`.
* ⚠ **RSS varies ±12% run to run.** One sample each; a <15% difference
  between modules is not evidence.
* ⚠ **`w` vs `wᶠ`, and `⊢wk` vs `⊢wkᶠ`.** `wᶠ` inserts a slot BELOW a
  family's own variable. **The count is wrong more often than the kind.**
* ⚠ **P1: eta covers every naturality lemma EXCEPT moving a family under
  a renaming.** `wᶠ-single`, `wᶠ¹/²/³-single`, `wᶠ-nrs`, `ren-wᶠ` and now
  `wᶠ-sub` each need a pointwise bridge. Budget one whenever a lemma
  moves a family under `extR`.
* ⚠ **`--safe` forbids `postulate`**, so there is no quiet way to skip an
  obligation. That is deliberate.
* ⚠ **Assert on every string replacement** if you generate modules — two
  silent no-op `replace`s once left a module claiming to be RED while green.
