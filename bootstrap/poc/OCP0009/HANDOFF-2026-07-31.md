## 1. ★ THE RULE THAT CHANGED, and what it invalidates

> **The POC owns its own syntax.** No dependence on `bootstrap/normalizer/**` —
> that is **another POC**, not shared infrastructure — and none on
> `formal/Once/**`. (PLAN §1.2.)

**Why it matters more than it sounds.** Borrowing a peer POC's object language
couples our design decisions to their accidental ones, and then *their limits
get recorded as our constraints*. That is not hypothetical — it happened this
session, twice, and cost real work:

- **W0d was scoped as "port the real IR to the linear core".** That framing is
  bottom-up by construction: it takes the existing target as authoritative and
  measures what the source can be squeezed into. Every wall it hit
  (`no ν`, `no Int`, `List Int is out`) got written down as a limit on the POC.
  None were findings about the right shape. The module that made the error
  (`NbEPLinIR`) is deleted.
- **Then the replacement made the same error one level down**, breaking the
  dependency but keeping the *vocabulary* — `Fix`/`fix`/`unfix`. `Fix` reads as
  a general fixpoint, which Once explicitly rejected (OCP-0003 removed
  `fold`/`unfold` from the IR). Caught by review, not by Agda.

**Corollary, worth internalising:** *"port the real X to Y" is almost always the
wrong scoping for POC work.* The port is downstream compiler work, gated on the
shape being settled.

**Second new standing constraint (PLAN §1.2): NO GENERAL FIXPOINT.** `μ`/`ν` are
the initial algebra and final coalgebra of a polynomial code, eliminated by
`cata` and one observation. No `fold`/`unfold`, no fixpoint combinator, no
`Hylo` as a primitive. Name things `Mu`/`Nu`/`inμ`/`outμ`/`inν` after Once's own
`In`/`out-μ`/`Out`/`in-ν`.

**Where the rule is still violated.** `NbEPLinPass`/`NbEPLinQTT` borrow a
cartesian SOURCE language (`Syntax.CCC.Term`) and `eval` from the normalizer
POC. That is *not* a mechanical fix — see §4.

## 3. THE TWO THINGS THAT WERE ACTUALLY HARD

### 3.1 `SN` under a binder — where W1c's strike was too broad

`sem-lam` takes `SN` of the lambda's BODY; `fund` only ever sees it closed up as
`s[σ,u]`. W1c's "Kripke is not needed" **held for the family** — the target
scope never grows — **but not for this premise**, and a renaming action on `⊩₁`
is not available to supply it:

> The structural transport `ren₁` is **BLOCKED** at `⊩₁Π`: the stored family
> offers instances only at `v = renTm ρ u`, the renamed clause demands one at
> every `v`, and the missing ones cannot be manufactured because **`⊩` is not
> total over `RTy`** (W1e/`SpikeSNK`) — so there is no fallback. The obvious
> weaker ask dies too: for `ρ = vs`, a `v : RTm (Ξ ∙)` may mention `vz`.
>
> ⚠ **ARGUED, not measured. NOT a refutation.** `ren₁` is not shown false, only
> unreachable by the induction. That is deliberately the right strength: the
> only decision it licenses is "do not build it", and it licenses that fully.

**The fix, and it costs the relation nothing:** instantiate the family at
`var x₀` (free by `CR3`); `s[single (var x₀)]` **is a renaming of `s`**; so
anti-renaming for `SN` recovers `SN s`. ~60 lines, mutual over `SNe`/`SN`/
`SNRed`. Hence `fund`'s `Var Ξ` argument, and hence `⊩ˢ-ren` (every renaming
substitution is reducible, by recursion on `⊢ctx`) as the way the induction
starts.

★ **THE FLIP CONDITION — this is what W2 needs.** Anti-renaming scales to new
formers because `sn-anti` is a lemma about RAW SYNTAX: it never mentions `⊩`, so
it is immune to whatever W2/W3 do to the relation, and sensitive only to `_⟶_`.
Cost is ~3 lines per new `RTm` constructor plus one commutation lemma per former
that BINDS and REDUCES. **One condition brings Kripke back: the kernel adopting
η-LONG normal forms** (βη equality, or W5 going NbE-shaped) — reify-at-a-fresh-
variable IS the Kripke step. Not met today: η lives in a separate `_≅η_`,
`⊢conv` uses the β-only `_≅ᵀ_`, and the committed route is reduction-based.

*(Aside for whoever costs βη: `NbEPDirDBEta`'s header defers Σ-η because `RTm`
lacks pair/projection constructors. W1g landed them. That blocker is stale.)*

### 3.2 `Free` does not fit in one recursion — W1e's finding, other line

`Free (A ⇒t B) f = (a : ⟦A⟧) → Free A a → …` puts `Free` **negatively**, and
that hypothesis is exactly what bounds `leval`, so it cannot go. Meanwhile
`Free` at `μt`/`νt` must be real and re-enters — so `FreeMu` occurs negatively
in its own definition. **Same wall as `SpikeSNK`'s "`⊩Π`'s function field puts
`⊩∋` negatively", from the opposite direction.** Same answer: stratify.

What makes it work: `FS G C` is built from `⊤`, base carriers, `Mu`, `Nu`, `⊎`,
`×` and contains **no function space, ever**, so a fixpoint payload's freedom
never needs the `⇒t` clause. Three layers — (1) `FreeMu`/`FreeNu` over the data
fragment only, arrow-free hence strictly positive; (2) `Free` as a plain
function structural in `LTy`; (3) `FreeFS G A` at an arbitrary carrier, bridged
to (1) by four one-line inductions. ⚠ **The carrier in (3) must be EXPLICIT** —
implicit, the `⊕f`/`⊗f` recursive calls leave it an unsolvable metavariable.

★★ **AND THE BORROWED CORE COULD NOT HAVE SHOWN IT.** `NbEPLinDyn` sets
`Free (μ F) x = ⊤`, justified by "a fixpoint holds no functions". True, and
beside the point: with `Kν` among the codes, **data can hold a PRODUCER**.
`box-not-free` is the concrete refutation — inductive data that is not free,
which `⊤` would have called free — and it is **not statable in the borrowed core
at all**, whose functor codes cannot put a `ν` inside a `μ`. Owning the syntax
is what made the counterexample expressible. That is the strongest single
argument for §1's rule.
