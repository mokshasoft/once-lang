## The results worth carrying forward

### 1. ★★★ HALF-GENERALIZATION IS THE WORST CASE

Measured on the 53-method `sz` tuple, cold, quiet box, back-to-back:

| | |
|---|---|
| specialize — 53 enumerated rungs | 147s |
| **half**-generalize — generic rung, enumerating consumer | 350s / OOM |
| generalize fully — method AND tuple computed from the description | **5s** |

A generic lemma is only generic **if its argument stays ABSTRACT at the
use site**. If the consumer enumerates, every rung instantiates the
generic proof at a CONCRETE argument and Agda unfolds it — the same
O(n²), with bigger terms. That is the proof-assistant twin of
monomorphization bloat, and in partial-evaluation terms the mistake is
generalizing over an argument that is still **static** at every use site.

⇒ before generalizing, ask *where the enumeration lives*. If it is in the
consumer, make the CONSUMER generic. `Lib/IFold` is that: the method is
computed from the `ICon`, the tuple from the `IDesc`, and no rung is ever
named.

### 2. ★★ THE MAP IS WHAT MAKES THE ENCODING A CLAIM

`Knot/Wf` says 53 rows are well formed. `Knot/Terms` says ONE term
encodes. Neither says the description IS the knot. **Measured**: set
`natrec`'s step field to `sucD 1` instead of `sucD 2` — `Knot/Wf` still
passes, `Knot/Ctors` still passes, `Knot/Map` fails. A wrong index is
well-formed AND inhabited AND has derivable constructors; only mapping
every Agda constructor through the table detects it.

### 3. ★ THE DEPTH MUST BE A NUMERAL, AND `Var` FORDS BOTH COMPONENTS

`num : ℕ → RTm Γ` is context-polymorphic, which is what lets its two
stability lemmas be STATED; for an opaque `d : RTm ⌊Δ⌋` they cannot be.
And §14's "Ford the component, not the pair" is needed in BOTH
components: `Var`'s `vz`/`vs` exist only at `suc m`, so they Ford the
depth as well as the tag. Dead ends recorded in `Knot/Build`.
