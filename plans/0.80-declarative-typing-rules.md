# Plan 0.80 — Take the ALGORITHM out of the typing rules

**Status:** **PHASE A LANDED** (2026-09-01, apex + three-arch green). Phase B
is open and is a plan of its own. **Relates:** D044, D045, D127, OCP-0006.
**Decision entry:** D134.

## 0. The understanding this plan is built on

`Once.Spec.Typing` IS `Once.TypeCheck.Judgment`, re-exported verbatim. So the
declarative typing judgment is the language definition — and seven of its
rules carry a premise that is not a typing fact but a CALL TO A DECISION
PROCEDURE:

    t-compose-check         composeMid ctx f g A ≡ just B
    t-app                   classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    t-effApp                classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    t-arg-driven-app-check  classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    t-cata-check            wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF
    t-In-app-check          wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF
    t-var-poly-instantiate-infer   isGround schema ≡ inj₁ g
    t-var-poly-instantiate         isGround schema ≡ inj₂ tt

**An algorithm in the denotational spec defeats the purpose of having a spec.**
The correctness statement must reference the spec — that is how correctness is
stated at all — but the spec must not reference the compiler's search
strategy, or "the compiler is correct" degenerates into "the compiler agrees
with itself".

Compose, denoted correctly, is one rule with the middle type EXISTENTIAL:

    Γ ⊢ f ⇐ B ⇒ C      Γ ⊢ g ⇐ A ⇒ B
    ─────────────────────────────────────
        Γ ⊢ compose f g ⇐ A ⇒ C

which is what `t-compose-check` already says once `composeMid` is deleted from
it — `B` is already an implicit. The premise adds no mathematical content; it
RESTRICTS the rule to the middle types one particular search can find.

## 1. What the algorithmic premises are actually buying

They are not free to remove, and the plan is honest about why.

Two distinct jobs are being done by these eight lines:

**(a) DECIDER-FOR-PROPERTY.** `wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF` stands in for
`WellFormedF F`; `isGround schema ≡ inj₁ g` stands in for `Ground schema`.
The property is already the mathematical premise and is already an implicit
argument of the rule. The equation only pins the witness to the decider's
output. Removing it costs proof-irrelevance for the property — and
`WellFormedF-irrelevant` / `IsBaseType-irrelevant` / `IsConcrete-irrelevant`
are ALREADY PROVEN in `Once.Functor.Translate`, while `Ground` is built from
`⊤`/`_×_`/`⊥` and is a proposition by construction.

**(b) DISAMBIGUATION.** `classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing` keeps `t-app` disjoint
from the builtin-head rules; `composeMid` pins a unique `B`. Their job is to
make derivations ESSENTIALLY UNIQUE — and this system defines the MEANING by
recursion on the derivation (`⟦_⟧ᶜ : ctx ⊢ᶜ e ∶ A ⨾ Ψ → …`, by direct
induction on `_⊢ᶜ_`). So derivation-uniqueness is currently what makes `⟦_⟧ᶜ`
well-defined without a coherence theorem, and what makes `check-complete`
hold by construction rather than as a theorem.

That is the trade D044/D045 made, and it is real. Removing (b) owes:

  * **Coherence** — two derivations of one judgment must denote the same
    Behavior. `RApp (RVar "id") x` would become derivable by BOTH `t-id-app`
    and `t-app`, denoting `morph-app IR.id ⟦x⟧` and `app ⟦id⟧ ⟦x⟧` — equal,
    but not definitionally.
  * **Completeness, restated** — `checkElab` becomes a decision procedure for
    a judgment it no longer mirrors exactly, so `check-complete` becomes a
    theorem about the fragment the search covers, not a definitional match.

## 2. The split, and the order

**Phase A — decider-elimination (4 sites, no ambiguity introduced). DONE.**

  A1. `t-cata-check` / `t-In-app-check`: `wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF`
      becomes the premise `WellFormedF F`.
  A2. `t-var-poly-instantiate-infer`: `isGround schema ≡ inj₁ g` becomes
      `Ground schema` (`g` is used by `extractGround`, so it stays a witness).
  A3. `t-var-poly-instantiate`: `isGround schema ≡ inj₂ tt` becomes
      `¬ (Ground schema)` — the honest positive statement of "not ground".
  A4. Follow the red. The elaborator supplies the decider's output as the
      witness at each site, so its side is a projection; the proofs that
      currently `rewrite` by the decider equation take the witness directly.
      `WellFormedF-irrelevant` closes any place two witnesses meet.

Phase A introduces NO ambiguity: the same set of judgments is derivable,
because the decider is sound and complete for its property. It is a pure
statement-cleanup, and it is the half that can be done without a coherence
theorem.

**What it actually took** (for calibration when Phase B is planned): the four
rule edits, a new `Once.TypeCheck.DeciderComplete` (the two directions of "the
decider decides", plus `Ground-irrelevant`), and SEVEN call-site edits —
`Realize` ×2, `Meaning` ×1, `MeaningBridge` ×2, `Elaborate` ×2,
`ElaborateProofs` ×3, `Completeness` ×6. Everything else in the cone
(`RealizeAgrees`, the whole Canon family, `CanonPolyTransport` and its reverse)
needed NO change at all: those proofs only pass the premise through, so
replacing an equation by a witness is invisible to them. `Once/Certified.agda`
and `Once/Adequacy/ArchCorrectness.agda` are green.

Two mechanical notes worth keeping. `Ground` is a defined function, not a
datatype, so `¬ (Ground _A)` does not determine `_A` — the schema has to be
passed explicitly at the two non-ground sites. And the witness/decider
identification is needed in BOTH directions, which is why
`isGround-complete-at` / `wellFormedF?-complete-at` (the "at the derivation's
own witness" forms) exist rather than just the plain completeness lemmas.

**Phase B — disambiguation-elimination (4 sites).** `classifyAppHead` off
`t-app`/`t-effApp`/`t-arg-driven-app-check`, `composeMid` off
`t-compose-check`. Owes the two obligations in §1(b).

**PROBED 2026-09-01 — both halves, with the cost now measured. Not landed.**

*`composeMid` half.* Removing the premise typechecks `Judgment` fine and the
mechanical sites are one-liners — the Canon family's `composeMid-canon` /
`-decanon` / `-polys-canon` / `-polys-decanon` transports all DELETE, which is
itself evidence the premise was carrying weight it should not have. The block
is `check-complete`. With the premise gone, its compose case must produce

    ⊢ᶜ f ∶ B ⇒ C  →  ⊢ᶜ g ∶ A ⇒ B  →  composeMid ctx f g A ≡ just B

and that is **FALSE**, with a counterexample already in the test suite:
`compose (\_ -> 0) (compose emit@E (\_ -> 42))` is well-typed and
`composeMid` returns `nothing` for it (before D135; after D135 the shape
merely moves). So `check-complete` cannot stay total once the rule is
declarative — which is exactly the D044/D045 trade, now measured rather than
predicted.

`check-complete` is LOAD-BEARING AT THE APEX (`ModuleComplete`, `Compile`,
`CanonAllFuns`, `CanonReflectAllFuns`), so weakening it to the elaborable
fragment changes what the compiler CLAIMS: "well-typed ⇒ compiles" becomes
"well-typed AND elaborable ⇒ compiles". That is arguably strictly better —
today the same restriction exists but is HIDDEN inside the language
definition — but it is an apex-level decision and needs to be taken
deliberately, not as a side effect. **That decision is what Phase B is
blocked on.**

*`classifyAppHead` half — ATTEMPTED 2026-09-01 and REVERTED. The premise is
NOT redundant, and the reason corrects §1(b)'s classification of it.*

The hypothesis was that the overlap it prevents is already prevented by the
sibling premise: a bare polymorphic builtin does not INFER, so
`ctx ⊢ᵢ f ∶ A ⇒ B` is underivable for the heads `classifyAppHead` excludes.
`Judgment` typechecks without it and all 59 call sites drop their argument
cleanly — but `check-complete` then fails, and the counterexample is
SHADOWING:

    fst : Int -> Int
    fst x = x
    test : Int
    test = fst 5

`classifyAppHead` is ctx-free, so it classifies `RApp (RVar "fst") 5` as the
BUILTIN regardless of the user's binding, and the elaborator rejects the
program (builtin `fst : A * B → A` does not take an `Int`). That rejection is
DELIBERATE and pinned by `TypeCheckSpec`'s "user-defined 'fst' is shadowed by
builtin". Remove the premise and `t-app` derives the program with the user's
`fst` — so the judgment would accept what the elaborator rejects, and
`check-complete` is false.

**So `classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing` is not disambiguation. It IS the language's
builtin-wins-over-shadowing rule, stated where it belongs.** Unlike
`wellFormedF?`/`isGround` (deciders standing in for properties, Phase A) and
`composeMid` (a search), this premise carries semantics, and it stays.

That leaves Phase B as ONE item — `composeMid` — blocked on the apex decision
above. Section §1(b) is corrected accordingly: of the four "disambiguation"
premises, three were semantics and one is a search.

## 3. What Phase B would settle downstream

`composeArgB` currently cannot recover the middle type of a constant-function
arm, so `compose (\_ -> 0) (compose emit@E (\_ -> 42))` does not typecheck
even though it is well-typed mathematically (plan 0.76 Phase E hit this: 3
`TraceSpec` failures). Today that is a LANGUAGE question, because `composeMid`
is in the rule. After Phase B it is a COMPLETENESS question about the
elaborator — improve the search, prove more programs reachable, no spec
change. That is the point of the plan.

## 4. Explicitly NOT in this plan

  * Moving `composeMid`/`classifyAppHead`/the lookups INTO the spec. That was
    considered and rejected: it sanctifies the algorithm instead of removing
    it. They stay in `Once.TypeCheck.Classify`, which is where an algorithm
    belongs.
  * The lookup premises (`lookupLocal ctx x ≡ nothing`, `lookupImport … ≡
    just T`) are NOT algorithmic. "This name is not shadowed by a local" and
    "the context assigns this name this type" are typing facts about the
    context, and the functions are its accessors. They stay.
  * `Once.Spec.Meaning`'s dependence on `Once.Compile` and the two Adequacy
    predicates (`meaningᵈ`'s indices). Separate question, separate plan.

## 5. P2 and P3 — the spec's re-export structure (assessed 2026-09-01)

### P2 — invert `Spec.Meaning`'s re-exports: LARGELY OBSOLETED BY P5

The idea was to move the six `Denotation` leaves under `Once/Spec/` so a
path-scoped diff of `formal/Once/Spec/` is complete by construction. That is
935 lines across six modules with ~50 distinct importers — a large mechanical
rename with no semantic content.

**P5 already bought most of its value**: `formal/scripts/spec-closure.py`
GENERATES the closure, and MERGE.md now diffs `$(python3
formal/scripts/spec-closure.py)`. The audit is complete without moving
anything; what P2 would add is that a NAIVE `git diff -- formal/Once/Spec/`
also works. Recommend NOT doing it, or doing it only alongside some other
reason to touch those files.

### P3 — `meaningᵈ`'s indices: TRACTABLE, and smaller than it looked

    meaningᵈ : (fmt) (m : C.Module) (mt : AS.ModuleTyped m)
             → MC.HasValidMain-decl m mt → Behavior

The worry was that the spec's meaning is indexed by the compiler and by two of
its own PROOF modules. Resolving where each name actually lives shrinks it:

  * `Module`, `FunInfo`, `PolyFunInfo`, `extractFunctions`, `extractAliases`
    are defined in **`Once.Parser` / `Once.Parser.Module.Core`** — program
    VOCABULARY, legitimately shared. `Once.Compile` only re-exports them.
    Nothing to fix; the theorem must talk about the same program.
  * Only SIX definitions are genuinely `Once.Compile`'s and reached by the
    meaning: `FunCtx`, `emptyFunCtx`, `extendFunCtx`, `buildPolyCtx`,
    `collectSigEffects`, `resolveFunType` — about 120 lines of module-scope
    bookkeeping, not pipeline.
  * `AllFunsTyped`, `ModuleTyped{,-ef}` (`Adequacy.AcceptSound`) and
    `MainExists`, `ModuleMain*-ef`, `HasValidMain-decl`
    (`Adequacy.ModuleComplete`) are SPEC NOTIONS — "what a well-typed module
    is", "what a valid `main` is" — that happen to be declared in proof
    modules. They are plain definitions; nothing about them depends on the
    proofs around them.

**The fix, and it is bounded:**

  1. New spec-owned module (say `Once/Spec/Program.agda`) holding the six
     scope definitions plus the five typed-module predicates.
  2. `Once.Compile` RE-EXPORTS the six, so its 30 importers are untouched;
     `AcceptSound` / `ModuleComplete` import the predicates rather than
     declaring them.
  3. `Once.Denotation.MainMeaning` then imports the spec module instead of
     `Once.Compile`, `Adequacy.AcceptSound` and `Adequacy.ModuleComplete` —
     and the spec's meaning stops reaching into the compiler and its proofs.

Step 2 is what keeps this non-breaking. The one judgement call is
`resolveFunType`: it resolves a DECLARED type against the context, which is
arguably elaboration rather than vocabulary. If it is elaboration, then "what
a well-typed module is" should not be phrased with it, and that is a real
question about the definition rather than a move — worth settling before
step 1 rather than after.
