# Plan 0.80 — Take the ALGORITHM out of the typing rules

**Status:** plan (2026-09-01). **Relates:** D044, D045, D127, OCP-0006.
**Owes a decision entry:** D134.

## 0. The understanding this plan is built on

`Once.Spec.Typing` IS `Once.TypeCheck.Judgment`, re-exported verbatim. So the
declarative typing judgment is the language definition — and seven of its
rules carry a premise that is not a typing fact but a CALL TO A DECISION
PROCEDURE:

    t-compose-check         composeMid ctx f g A ≡ just B
    t-app                   classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    t-effApp                classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    t-arg-driven-app-check  classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    t-cata-check            wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF
    t-In-app-check          wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF
    t-var-poly-instantiate-infer   isGround schema ≡ inj₁ g
    t-var-poly-instantiate         isGround schema ≡ inj₂ tt

**An algorithm in the denotational spec defeats the purpose of having a spec.**
The correctness statement must reference the spec — that is how correctness is
stated at all — but the spec must not reference the compiler's search
strategy, or "the compiler is correct" degenerates into "the compiler agrees
with itself".

Compose, denoted correctly, is one rule with the middle type EXISTENTIAL:

    Γ ⊢ f ⇐ B ⇒ C      Γ ⊢ g ⇐ A ⇒ B
    ─────────────────────────────────────
        Γ ⊢ compose f g ⇐ A ⇒ C

which is what `t-compose-check` already says once `composeMid` is deleted from
it — `B` is already an implicit. The premise adds no mathematical content; it
RESTRICTS the rule to the middle types one particular search can find.

## 1. What the algorithmic premises are actually buying

They are not free to remove, and the plan is honest about why.

Two distinct jobs are being done by these eight lines:

**(a) DECIDER-FOR-PROPERTY.** `wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF` stands in for
`WellFormedF F`; `isGround schema ≡ inj₁ g` stands in for `Ground schema`.
The property is already the mathematical premise and is already an implicit
argument of the rule. The equation only pins the witness to the decider's
output. Removing it costs proof-irrelevance for the property — and
`WellFormedF-irrelevant` / `IsBaseType-irrelevant` / `IsConcrete-irrelevant`
are ALREADY PROVEN in `Once.Functor.Translate`, while `Ground` is built from
`⊤`/`_×_`/`⊥` and is a proposition by construction.

**(b) DISAMBIGUATION.** `classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing` keeps `t-app` disjoint
from the builtin-head rules; `composeMid` pins a unique `B`. Their job is to
make derivations ESSENTIALLY UNIQUE — and this system defines the MEANING by
recursion on the derivation (`⟦_⟧ᶜ : ctx ⊢ᶜ e ∶ A ⨾ Ψ → …`, by direct
induction on `_⊢ᶜ_`). So derivation-uniqueness is currently what makes `⟦_⟧ᶜ`
well-defined without a coherence theorem, and what makes `check-complete`
hold by construction rather than as a theorem.

That is the trade D044/D045 made, and it is real. Removing (b) owes:

  * **Coherence** — two derivations of one judgment must denote the same
    Behavior. `RApp (RVar "id") x` would become derivable by BOTH `t-id-app`
    and `t-app`, denoting `morph-app IR.id ⟦x⟧` and `app ⟦id⟧ ⟦x⟧` — equal,
    but not definitionally.
  * **Completeness, restated** — `checkElab` becomes a decision procedure for
    a judgment it no longer mirrors exactly, so `check-complete` becomes a
    theorem about the fragment the search covers, not a definitional match.

## 2. The split, and the order

**Phase A — decider-elimination (4 sites, no ambiguity introduced).**

  A1. `t-cata-check` / `t-In-app-check`: `wellFormedF? F ≡ just wfF`
      becomes the premise `WellFormedF F`.
  A2. `t-var-poly-instantiate-infer`: `isGround schema ≡ inj₁ g` becomes
      `Ground schema` (`g` is used by `extractGround`, so it stays a witness).
  A3. `t-var-poly-instantiate`: `isGround schema ≡ inj₂ tt` becomes
      `¬ (Ground schema)` — the honest positive statement of "not ground".
  A4. Follow the red. The elaborator supplies the decider's output as the
      witness at each site, so its side is a projection; the proofs that
      currently `rewrite` by the decider equation take the witness directly.
      `WellFormedF-irrelevant` closes any place two witnesses meet.

Phase A introduces NO ambiguity: the same set of judgments is derivable,
because the decider is sound and complete for its property. It is a pure
statement-cleanup, and it is the half that can be done without a coherence
theorem.

**Phase B — disambiguation-elimination (4 sites).** `classifyAppHead` off
`t-app`/`t-effApp`/`t-arg-driven-app-check`, `composeMid` off
`t-compose-check`. Owes the two obligations in §1(b). This is a plan of its
own once Phase A is in; do NOT start it inside Phase A.

## 3. What Phase B would settle downstream

`composeArgB` currently cannot recover the middle type of a constant-function
arm, so `compose (\_ -> 0) (compose emit@E (\_ -> 42))` does not typecheck
even though it is well-typed mathematically (plan 0.76 Phase E hit this: 3
`TraceSpec` failures). Today that is a LANGUAGE question, because `composeMid`
is in the rule. After Phase B it is a COMPLETENESS question about the
elaborator — improve the search, prove more programs reachable, no spec
change. That is the point of the plan.

## 4. Explicitly NOT in this plan

  * Moving `composeMid`/`classifyAppHead`/the lookups INTO the spec. That was
    considered and rejected: it sanctifies the algorithm instead of removing
    it. They stay in `Once.TypeCheck.Classify`, which is where an algorithm
    belongs.
  * The lookup premises (`lookupLocal ctx x ≡ nothing`, `lookupImport … ≡
    just T`) are NOT algorithmic. "This name is not shadowed by a local" and
    "the context assigns this name this type" are typing facts about the
    context, and the functions are its accessors. They stay.
  * `Once.Spec.Meaning`'s dependence on `Once.Compile` and the two Adequacy
    predicates (`meaningᵈ`'s indices). Separate question, separate plan.
