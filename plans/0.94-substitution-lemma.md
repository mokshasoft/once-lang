# Plan 0.94 — The judgment must not distinguish a definition from its definiens

## 0. The one property

> **`let x = e in b` and a top-level `x ≝ e` used in `b` must be
> interderivable.**

Once's thesis is that programs are mathematical statements. A type system in
which a definition and its definiens are not interchangeable is not stating
mathematics — it is stating facts about the spelling of the program.

The metatheoretic name is the **substitution lemma**, with its module-level
twin, closure under **δ-unfolding**:

        Γ ⊢ᵢ e ∶ A     Γ , x∶A ⊢ᶜ b ∶ B                 u ≝ e
        ───────────────────────────────         ─────────────────────────
              Γ ⊢ᶜ b[e/x] ∶ B                    ⊢ b[u]  ⟺  ⊢ b[e/x]

Neither holds today.

## 1. The measurement — verified 2026-09-19

Machine-checked against the built compiler. Same `u`, same type; the ONLY
difference is top-level name vs. let-bound variable:

```once
u : IO Unit                                                    -- top-level
u = emit@E 9
f = compose exit@S (compose (\_ -> 7) u)                       -- Typecheck OK

f = let u = emit@E 9 in compose exit@S (compose (\_ -> 7) u)   -- REJECTED
```

`let u = emit@E 9 in u` infers fine on its own, so the binding is not at fault.
It is not about effects either: `let g = id in compose id g` fails identically.

### The cause

`Judgment.agda` has **four premises that compute on syntax**:

    Judgment.agda:559   t-compose-check          composeMid ctx f g A ≡ just B
    Judgment.agda:467   t-app                    classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    Judgment.agda:484   t-effApp                 classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing
    Judgment.agda:734   t-arg-driven-app-check   classifyAppHead f ≡ nothing

and imports them at `Judgment.agda:60-65`:

```agda
open import Once.TypeCheck.Classify
  using (NamedCtx; lookupLocal; lookupImport; lookupPoly; lookupPolyPrefix;
         removePoly;
         ctxWithImportsAndPolys; extendNamedCtx; classifyAppHead;
         composeArgB; composeMid)
```

**That import line is the defect.** A premise that inspects syntax cannot be
stable under substitution, because substitution changes syntax.

* `composeMid`/`composeArgB` (`Classify.agda`) is a *syntactic partial
  synthesizer*: it matches the argument's SHAPE — `RInt`, `RLam _ (RInt _)`,
  `RLam _ (RFloat …)`, `RLam _ (RStringLit _)`, `RLam _ RUnit`, a nested
  `compose`, or a name — to guess the middle type. `composeArgB-lookup`
  (`Classify.agda:309`) consults `NamedCtx.polys` and `NamedCtx.imports` and
  **never the local context**, which is exactly why a top-level `u` is found
  and a `let`-bound `u` is not.
* `classifyAppHead` is **ctx-free**: it classifies `RApp (RVar "fst") 5` as the
  builtin regardless of a user binding. Plan 0.80 documents this as a
  CONFIRMED BUG inherited from the unfinished generator-namespace migration.

`Classify.agda` states the defect against itself:

> "It stays a LITERAL enumeration for the reason `composeArgB` is a syntactic
> partial synthesizer at all: it sits below the judgment (`t-compose-check`
> names `composeMid`), so it **cannot consult inference**. Plan 0.80 Phase B is
> what would let this be a real synthesis question instead."

## 2. The rule

**Every premise of a typing rule must be a judgment, not a computation on
syntax.**

This is plan 0.80's thesis. 0.80 Phase A landed the deciders-standing-in-for-
properties half (`wellFormedF?`, `isGround`). These four are Phase B, and they
are the remaining violations.

## 3. Why this is not just plan 0.80 again

Plan 0.80 framed Phase B as a **trade**: delete the premise, `check-complete`
goes false, "well-typed ⇒ compiles" weakens to "well-typed AND elaborable ⇒
compiles", and that is an apex decision it is blocked on.

That measures *deleting* the premise. The third option 0.80 does not list is
**replacing** it:

    ctx ⊢ᶜ f ∶ (B ⇒[π] C)   →   ctx ⊢ᶜ g ∶ (A ⇒[π] B)
    ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    ctx ⊢ᶜ compose f g ∶ (A ⇒[π] C)

declarative in the judgment, with the *elaborator* gaining a
**domain-given / codomain-synthesised** checker — the application/spine mode of
bidirectional typing. `check-complete` then stays true, because the elaborator
can actually find what the rule asserts.

Measured 2026-09-19: plain inference covers only **3 of 10** shapes (`42`, a
top-level name, an applied SigOp — lambdas and bare polymorphic builtins do not
infer). But `compose f g : A ⇒ C` **knows `A`**, and with the domain given all
five failing shapes check:

| shape | infer alone | domain given |
|---|---|---|
| `\_ -> 42`, `\y -> y` | no | **checks** |
| `id`, `fst` | no | **checks** |
| nested `compose` | no | **checks** |

Strictly stronger than `composeArgB`: `\y -> y` works, which the eight-shape
enumeration cannot express at all.

## 4. The known risk

**The middle type is not unique.** `initial : Void ⇒ B` checks at `Int` *and*
at `Float` (both verified). So once the premise is gone, `compose f initial`
has a derivation at every `B`, and `check-complete` becomes "the elaborator
finds *a* `B` that makes the rest check", not "*the* `B`".

POC this single case before committing to the rest.

## 5. Prerequisites — and why this plan is not startable today

1. **`RawExpr` substitution does not exist.** No `_[_/_]`, no `substRaw`,
   no `renameRaw` anywhere in `formal/`. The lemma cannot be *stated* without
   first building capture-avoiding substitution on `RawExpr`, which binds in
   at least `RLam`, `RLet` and the `case`/destruct forms. Cost UNKNOWN —
   being measured.
2. **Three of the four premises are blocked on plan 0.50.** Plan 0.80 attempted
   removing `classifyAppHead` and REVERTED: without it the judgment derives
   programs the elaborator rejects (the `fst` shadowing case, pinned by
   `TypeCheckSpec`). 0.80's finding: the premise is load-bearing *only because*
   generators and user names still share one identity space, and plan 0.50
   dissolves that. So `classifyAppHead` is BLOCKED ON 0.50, not on this plan.

## 6. Ordering

    A. POC the `initial` / B-non-uniqueness case.                      (½ day)
    B. Delete `composeMid`'s premise; delete `composeArgB`/`composeMid`
       and the four Canon transports (0.80 measured those as free).
    C. Write the domain-given checker; close `check-complete`.          (the week)
    D. Cost `RawExpr` substitution; decide whether to state the general
       lemma or a cheaper equivalent (a "let = def" theorem on the
       ELABORATION rather than on raw syntax).
    E. `classifyAppHead` — after plan 0.50.

**The gate already exists and does not need inventing.** `check-complete` says
"well-typed ⇒ elaborable" and is load-bearing at the apex (`ModuleComplete`,
`Compile`, `CanonAllFuns`, `CanonReflectAllFuns`). Deleting the premise makes
the judgment strictly bigger, so `check-complete` goes red *precisely* where
the elaborator is too weak. Close it and the property is proved.

## 7. A cheap structural guard, worth doing regardless

The violation is visible in one grep:

    Judgment.agda imports a computation on RawExpr from Classify.agda

Make that a checked rule and this class cannot reappear silently. It would have
caught both offenders at the import line, long before either surfaced as
`let ≠ def`.
