## 3. Scoping the bridges (items 1–3), 2026-10-20

Two read-only passes (no build). Both bridges are **false today**, for reasons that are fixed before any
bridge proof is written.

### 3.1 Why `complete` is false: signature-less definitions
`w-nosig` (`Spec/Surface/Program.agda:208`) lets a definition without a signature take ANY schema that is
principal over its own domains, i.e. its domains may be chosen to suit later uses. `twice = \f -> f (f 3)`
is principal at both `(Int -> Int) -> Int` and `(Int -> Void) -> Void` (incomparable, D322 §5), so
`h : (Int -> Void) -> Void; h = \k -> twice k` is a Spec program the compiler (one schema per definition,
by unification) rejects. No compiler that fixes one schema can match it, and an oracle hypothesis is
refutable (it would make the apex vacuous). Options:
- **(c1) without a signature, a definition has the type its body SYNTHESIZES** (`L ⊢ e ⇒ A`, still
  `Principal`). Synthesis is deterministic (`synth-unique`); the compiler uses `inferElab` only, the D072
  oracle leaves the trust boundary (it may stay as a suggested-signature hint), plan 0.59's "scope grew"
  hole closes. Cost: a body that only checks (a lambda, `f x = …`, or a bare polymorphic reference) needs a
  signature; ~4 of 153 test programs. Amends D007/D072. Binder annotations (D322's future extension) bring
  signature-less lambdas back deterministically later.
- **(c2) Hindley–Milner most-general schema** under equality typing at domains: keeps signature-less
  functions, but needs a declarative HM layer in the Spec and verified unification (plan 0.59 P1–P3), roughly
  +3,000–6,000 lines.
- Rejected: verified inference for the Spec as it stands (the property is false); a conditional apex.

### 3.2 Why `sound` is false: compiler divergences (all compiler-side fixes)
- **R1** `extractFunctions` silently drops a definition whose name differs from the pending signature, and
  ignores dangling/duplicate signatures (`Parser.agda:330,334,369`); the Spec's `Walk` rejects them.
- **R2** `main` is put in scope at `Eff Unit Unit` (`Lang/Module.agda:113`); the Spec gives it its own type
  and converts only at the entry. Keep `main` at its type, convert at the entry (also closes S10).
- **R3** the program's own polymorphic names are resolved bare and BEFORE generators and imports
  (`Resolve.agda:224`); the Spec resolves generators, then imports, then definitions, to `canonical [x]`.
- **R4** imported signatures sit at the import's position; the Spec's signature is `Σ₀ ++ Σᵢ` (own first),
  and `sound` asks for `exec … ≡ …`. The compiler adopts the Spec's order.
- **R5** imported modules are only parsed, and the compiler's grammar still accepts string literals, which
  the Spec's grammar does not (string support went with plan 0.106). The parser rejects them.
- **R7** (found 2026-10-21, building the front-end bridge) `main`'s body is checked only at `Eff Unit Unit`
  (`entryType`), never at `main`'s own type: `main : Unit -> Unit; main = tick` with `tick : Act Unit Unit`
  is accepted (`once check`: OK) though the Spec rejects it (the body does not check at the pure type). Fix,
  compiler-side, the Spec's shape (D315): `main` is checked and compiled at its own type `A <: Eff Unit Unit`
  like every definition; the core program's entry converts (`runEntry` along `A <: IOUnit`), and the
  binary's entry calls `main` (`initial ∘ Call main` when `A`'s codomain is `Void`).
- **R6** (to confirm) `Lang.Principal` assumes checking is closed upward along `<:`, unproved; the
  exact-shape generator rules (`c-id`, `c-poly`) are suspects. POC first.

### 3.5 The meaning relation
**Done (2026-10-21), `Adequacy.SpecCore`:** `Poly.trD` translates the compiler's polymorphic core derivations
(the telescope's bodies) into the Spec's; `Cases.fundamental` proves that a derivation instantiated and its
translation mean related computations at related instances, environments and definitions environments, for
every core rule (modules `Index`, `Value`, `Monad`, `Env`, `Conv`, `Layer`, `Fold`, `Unfold`, `Cases`;
no postulate, no `with`). Its hypotheses: related definitions environments (`DefsR`, discharged by
induction on the telescope) and related declarations (`SigR`, from `SpecInterp.toImpl`).
Also done: the telescope (`Program.teleS`, `teleR`: definitions environments related, given `SigG`) and the
observation (`Observe.observe-R`: related trees have the same trace, given `Agree` between the
interpretation and the world). Next: the program's entry, `SigG`/`Agree` for `SpecInterp.toInterp`, then the
front end (the Spec program `Denotes` the source and its telescope is `teleS` of the compiler's).

Design notes, as built:
A logical relation at ground types (`TyR ∅ₖ A A′`), against a Spec Interpretation `J` and the compiler's
implementation read from it (`SpecInterp.toImpl`):
- values: `⊤`/`⊥`/the same word at base types; pairwise at `*`, same side at `+`; at an arrow, related
  arguments go to related computations (`Zero`: no argument); `μ`: the compiler's `Val` data against `μS
  (code F)`, layer by layer; `ν` at `pure`/`act`: coinductive, layer by layer (blocks at `act`).
- computations at a grade: `pure` the value relation; `act` trees — `ret`/`done`, a compiler `call` to the
  Spec `call` of the same declaration (arguments and answers through `toB`/`fromB`), a compiler `halt` to a
  Spec call whose answer is `void`; `eff` the same, coinductively (`T∞` on both sides).
- the fundamental lemma over `TmR`: related environments give related meanings, one case per core rule; a
  `sigop` is the declaration's contract on both sides (`pureAt`/`answerAt`), a `ref` the definitions
  environments' related entries.
- risk, to prove first: `μ` (two encodings of initial algebras) and the `T∞` loop (`iter`), each a small
  POC before the full case list.
  - `μ` POC (2026-10-20): the relation is definable — base values constructor by constructor (`BR` over
    `TyR`), a layer relation by recursion on `FunR` (`LayerR f R`), and `data μR f` with
    `inR : LayerR f (μR f) x y → μR f ⟨ x ⟩ (inS y)` passes Agda's positivity check through `LayerR`.
  - loop POC (2026-10-20): holds. A tree relation `TR R` (ret/ret; a call to the related Spec call,
    related arguments, continuations related on related answers; `halt` against any Spec call), its
    coinductive closure `T∞R R` (`force∞ : TR (SumR R (T∞R R)) …`), and `loopR`: a related round gives
    related loops, by a copattern step (`force∞ (loopR r) = blockR (h r)`) over an inductive walk of the
    block (`blockR`, calling `loopR` under `ret`) — accepted by the termination checker under
    `--guardedness`, the shape of the Spec's own `anaA`/`treeA`.
  - Open in the relation: which compiler `CallOp`/`HaltOp` a Spec `Op` is (from `toISig`), and the answer
    relation (`toB`/`fromB`).
