# Plan 0.143 — Every law of the language applies to some program

## Summary

The language definition makes promises about itself: laws such as "composing with the identity changes
nothing" or "a polymorphic definition commutes with map". Each is proved. But a promise can be proved
and still say nothing, if it only speaks about situations that never happen; then the proof is
worthless and no gate notices. This plan adds, next to the promises, one concrete example for each
law in which everything the law assumes really holds, so that "the law is proved" also means "the law
is about something". When it is done, a reviewer no longer has to check each law's assumptions by eye
to know that it is not empty.

## 1. Why

A law in `Once.Spec.Metatheory` is a Π-type: given derivations and side conditions, the two sides are
the same. It is vacuous when its premises can never be met together, and then every gate passes:
- it happened: D369's first `cata-uniqueᶜ` had its hypothesis at a free usage, where a context entry no
  environment fills (`x : Void` at `[One]`) makes it hold for any `h`. Review caught it, not a gate
  (`SpecTests.Metatheory.Fold` now refutes that form);
- the ways it can happen: a side condition no term meets (`Pure` at a grade nothing has), a law whose
  two sides never type together (a combinator that does not type at the law's type), a relation `_≅_`
  whose quantifier over environments is empty, or a hypothesis on definitions (`SameDefs`,
  `ParamDefs`) no meaning satisfies.

What guards against this today, claim by claim: `Inhabited` (worlds and models exist), `param-tele`
(programs meet `ParamDefs`), `observes` (sameness is not everything), and the `SpecTests` refutations
(each exhibits definitions, an instance and an environment meeting `≅`'s hypotheses). Nothing covers the
36 laws of `Categorical`/`Equations` or the rest uniformly.

The reviewer's question (2026-11-05): a reader of Once should only need the Spec to know what the
language means. The laws are in the Spec (`Once.Spec.Metatheory`), but as promises about the meaning,
not part of it: a vacuous law does not change what any program means, it only makes a promise hollow.
Today, trusting the promises means reading every law's hypotheses and judging them satisfiable. With
this plan, the Spec states that each law has an instance, and the proof provides it; the reviewer reads
the statement of the instance requirement once, not each law's satisfiability.

  (`Laws.CataLaws.cataTy`, `Laws.MapTop.mapTy`) but never build them.

## 3. Decisions

- **L1, what an instance must show — OPEN.** Recommendation: for each law, one concrete choice of all
  its arguments such that (a) every hypothesis holds; (b) the context is empty (`Γ = ∅`, so the one
  environment exists and `_≅_` quantifies over something: the D369 failure mode is excluded by
  construction); (c) the two sides are different terms. (b) is what makes the instance non-degenerate;
  (c) keeps an instance from being `refl` in disguise. Alternative: (a) only — cheaper, but (a) alone
  admits D369's vacuous instance.
- **L2, where it is stated — OPEN.** Recommendation: the requirement in `Once.Spec.Metatheory` (a record
  `LawsApply`, one field per law, each an `∃` of the arguments with (a)–(c)), proved under
  `Once/Metatheory` and carried by `CertifiedBuild.language`, like every claim (D304). Alternative:
  witnesses in `SpecTests` only — not carried by the apex, and the reviewer would have to read them.
- **L3, scope — OPEN.** Recommendation: every claim with premises beyond its type indices: the 36 laws,
  `parametricity` (its `ParamDefs` hypothesis is already met by `param-tele`; `naturality` needs an
  instance with `F`, `G` strictly positive), `coherence.observes`, the `term-model` and `recursion`
  claims with side conditions. Claims that quantify only over types or computations (`types`,
  `effects`) need none.
- **L4, the merge rule — OPEN (MERGE.md is yours to change).** Proposed wording for MERGE.md §1, *The
  Spec*: "A new law or claim in `Once.Spec.Metatheory` comes with its instance in `LawsApply` (plan
  0.143); a law whose premises cannot be instantiated at an empty context does not merge."

## 4. Steps

1. Spec patch (for approval): `LawsApply` in `Once.Spec.Metatheory` per L1–L3, a field in `Metatheory`.
   A D-entry; `language.md` if L4 is taken.
2. A derivation kit under `Once/Metatheory/Instances/`: typed derivations at `Γ = ∅` of the
   combinators (`idᶜ`, `composeᶜ`, `pairᶜ`, `caseᶜ`, `curryᶜ`, `cataᶜ`, `anaᶜ`, `mapᶜ`, `layerᶜ`, …) at
   small concrete types, generalizing the `SpecTests` helpers (explicit usages where they do not
   reduce at an abstract length; explicit `wk` arguments, which unification cannot invert).
3. Instances for `Categorical` and `Equations` (36), each by construction; the side conditions by
   `Pure` derivations at grade `pure`.
4. Instances for `parametricity`, `coherence`, `term-model`, `recursion`.
5. Wire into `Once.Metatheory`; `make certified`; gates.
6. The `SpecTests` witnesses stay (they show each restriction is needed: not too weak); `LawsApply`
   shows each law applies (not empty). D-entry records both sides.

## 5. Done when

`Once.Spec.Metatheory` states that every law listed in L3 has an instance at an empty context with its
premises met and different sides; it is proved without postulates and carried by `CertifiedBuild`. A law added later
needs a matching field by hand: the build cannot see a law with no field, so that part is the merge
rule's (L4), checked at review.
