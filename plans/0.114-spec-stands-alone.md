# Plan 0.114 — The Spec stands on its own

## Goal

Everything else in the development depends on the Spec: the compiler is proved correct against
it, the metatheory is about it, and a reader trusts it instead of the compiler. So it is held to
a higher standard than the rest of the code. When this plan is done:

* **the Spec is the directory `Once/Spec`**. A reviewer reads exactly those files, and a path
  filter (`git diff -- formal/Once/Spec formal/Once/Spec.agda`) shows every change to the
  language definition;
* **it depends on nothing but itself and the Agda standard library**;
* **every name is imported from the module that defines it, under the name it was given there**;
* **it is a definition, not a development**: no proofs beyond what a definition needs to type-check,
  no deciders, no implementation;
* **it reads as a mathematical text** for someone who knows categorical semantics (see
  "Target structure").

## The rules

Every module under `Once/Spec` (and `Once/Spec.agda`) obeys all of these. `make spec-gate`
checks R1–R4 against a shrinking baseline. R5 is checked by Agda. R6–R10 are review rules (§
Review).

* **R1 — Imports.** Only `Once.Spec.*` and the Agda standard library. No module from the
  implementation, however small or "shared" (`Once.Type`, `Once.Word`, `Once.CanonicalName`,
  `Once.Parser.Token`, `Once.Semantics.Value`, …). What the Spec needs from such a module is
  moved into the Spec, and the implementation imports it from there.
* **R2 — No re-exports.** No `public` anywhere: not `open import M public`, and not
  `open R public` on a record either (use `R.field`, or `open R` locally in the client).
  `Once.Spec` is an index that imports its modules and exports nothing.
* **R3 — No renaming, no hiding.** No `renaming (…)` and no `hiding (…)` on any import. A clash is
  resolved by a qualified import (`import Data.String as String`, then `String._≟_`). This
  aliases the module, not the name: the reader still sees the name as defined. Or the clash is
  removed at its source by choosing a better name.
* **R4 — Explicit imports.** Every `open import` has a `using (…)` list. Every name a module
  uses can be traced to its definition by reading the module's header.
* **R5 — `--safe`.** Every Spec module starts with `{-# OPTIONS --safe #-}`. Agda then rejects
  postulates, `TERMINATING`, `NO_POSITIVITY_CHECK` and the rest in the Spec and in everything it
  imports. (Compatible with the library flags `--exact-split --guardedness`.)
* **R6 — Definitions only.** Inductive definitions, records, relations and total functions that
  are the meaning. A lemma stays only if a definition needs it in order to type-check (a
  coercion, an index equation). Laws are metatheory: the statement goes in `Spec.Metatheory`, the
  proof under `Once/Metatheory`. Deciders (`_≟_`, `…?`), `show` functions, error reporting and
  `Bool`-valued tests are implementation, unless the language is defined by them, and then the
  header says why.
* **R7 — No borrowing.** No definition is phrased with compiler helpers. If the compiler
  computes a thing (a resolved type, a parsed declaration, a lowered primitive), the Spec says
  what the thing *is*, and a bridge proves that the compiler computes it. The known borrowings
  are listed below.
* **R8 — One concept per module**, named for the concept, in the place the target structure
  gives it.
* **R9 — Headers say what is defined.** No plan numbers, decision numbers, history or "moved
  from". The decision log keeps the history.
* **R10 — Names are the language's names.** A notation a reader would expect (`⟦_⟧`, `_⊢_∶_`,
  `μ`, `ν`, `T`) over suffix variants (`ᵛ`, `ᵈ`, `ᶜᵗ`, `≟str`). Plan 0.112's renames apply to the
  Spec first.

## Target structure

Once is the internal language of a category. The Spec should present it in the order a
categorical semanticist expects:
1. the objects;
2. the signature of generators;
3. the calculus that presents the free structure on that signature;
4. its models, and the interpretation functor out of the syntax;
5. what is observed;
6. the concrete syntax that elaborates into the calculus;
7. what a correct compiler is;
8. the laws.

The module names below are the target. Each phase moves toward it.

| Part | Content | Today |
|---|---|---|
| `Spec.Type` | The objects: `1`, `0`, `×`, `+`, the graded exponential `A ⇒[q,π] B`, `μ F`, `ν F` over strictly positive functors `F`. The grades: the usage semiring `{0, 1, ω}` and the effect order `pure ≤ eff`. Subtyping `<:` as a thin category (at most one coercion between two types). | `Once.Type` (831 lines, with deciders and `show`), `Once.Type.Sub`, the functor part of `Once.Functor.Translate` |
| `Spec.Signature` | Σ: the generators (the `signature` declarations), each with its type and the contract it owes. | `Spec.Contract`'s `Key`/`ISig`/`Contract`, `Once.CanonicalName` |
| `Spec.Core.*` | The calculus: terms in context (de Bruijn), the graded judgment `Γ ⊢[Ψ] t ∷ A ! π`, renaming and substitution (composition in the syntactic category), the categorical combinators as derived terms, type variables and the telescope of definitions. | `Spec.Core.{Syntax, Typing, Rename, Subst, Derived, PolyTy, PolyTyping, TySubst, Telescope}` (some carry lemmas, R6) |
| `Spec.Meaning.*` | The models: a type denotes a set `⟦A⟧`; a pure morphism is a function, an effectful one a Kleisli arrow of the interaction-tree monad `T` over Σ; `μ`/`ν` are the initial algebra and final coalgebra (`cata`, `ana`, `in`, `out`). | `Spec.Program` | A program: a module as a telescope of typed definitions, a core program; its meaning, `main` run in the telescope's environment. | `Spec.Core.Translate`, `Spec.Module`, `Spec.Program`, `Spec.Core.Telescope` |
| `Spec.Surface.*` | The concrete language: lexing, the declaration grammar, parsing, name resolution, the surface terms and their bidirectional typing, and elaboration into the calculus. | `Spec.{Lexing, Parsing, Grammar.*, Resolution, Syntax, Typing, Elaboration}`, `TypeCheck.{Raw, Judgment}`, `Spec.Core.{Abstract, AbsTy, Schema}` |
| `Spec.Correct` | The criterion: a compiler is correct when, for every interpretation, a program it accepts has the behaviour the Spec gives it, and it accepts every program the Spec types (and the target admits). | `Once.Adequacy.CorrectCompiler` |
| `Spec.Metatheory` | The laws, as statements: the term model is a category and `⟦_⟧` a functor out of it (graded); substitution is typed; β for `let`; subtyping is a partial order with unique derivations; the judgment's mode agreement; later coherence, the monad and domain laws, and uniqueness of `cata`/`ana`. | `Spec.Metatheory`, `Spec.Core.TermModel` |

Whether "Surface" belongs inside the Spec at all is a fair question: categorically, the core is
the language and the surface is a presentation of it. It stays, because `correct` is stated about
source text. But it is a separate part, and nothing in the core or the meaning imports it.

## The borrowings (R7): where the Spec calls compiler code today

1. **Monomorphic definitions.** `Spec/Module.agda:100` `mono` takes a definition's type from
   `C.resolveFunType … ≡ inj₂ ty`. `ModuleTyped` ranges over
   `Parser.extractFunctions (extractAliases m)`.
   Target: with a signature, a `⊢ᶜ` derivation at it; without one, any `ty` with a derivation.
2. **Module → program.** `Spec/Core/Translate.agda` builds on `Compile.FunCtx`/`buildPolyCtx`,
   `Parser.FunInfo` and `Classify.lookupImport`, and picks "the first `main`, as the compiler's
   `findMain`". Target: over the Spec's own scope and telescope; `main` by its unique name.
3. **The meaning's laws** go through the compiler IR (`Adequacy/Compile.agda:515`,
   `behavior-by (⟦ moduleToProgram … ⟧IR …)`). Target: from `runProgram` directly. DONE (2026-10-13):
   every `T∞` run is a prefix family (`Totality.observable∞`, `events∞-pf`), so `⟦_⟧ᵈᴵ` is
   `mkBehavior` of the core run with its own laws (`Compile.runProgram-pf`).
4. **`t-var-own`'s negative premise** `lookupImport sig x ≡ nothing` (`Judgment.agda:164`).
   Target: generators and definitions disjoint by construction.
5. **`sigOpRefᵛ`** is built with `Arith.SigOp.Builders.arrow-info` and `DenotTrace.sigOpT`.
   Target: from the contract alone. DONE for effectful arrows (2026-10-13): an `eff`/`eff⁺`/`prod`
   reference is the generic effect `opCall⁺` read at its grade (D311); the pure ones read the
   implementation (`valueOf`). The compiler's `sigOpT` appears only in the bridges.
6. **Surface `case` charges `Ψ₁ + Ψ₂`; the core charges one `Ψ` for both arms** (the additive
   `&`). Target: the surface rule as the core's. This relaxes the language and needs a decision
   entry.
7. **`d-lam`'s premise `(q' ≤q Many) ≡ true`** is always true. Delete it. DONE (2026-10-12):
   deleted; the elaboration reads `≤q-Many q'` (ω is the top usage, `Spec.Type.Grade`).
8. **The core's primitive meanings are right but live in the compiler.** `+` means the target
   CPU's `add` on an `int-bits`-wide word (D054). `/` and `%` are total, with RISC-V semantics
   (D055). `Float` means the target's IEEE operation, not an exact value (D113). That is the
   language, and it belongs in the Spec: the target's numeric structure (`TargetNum`) is a
   parameter of the model, and the base types are interpreted in it.
   What is wrong is only the route. `Spec/Core/Meaning.agda:83` defines
   `primSem p-add = semP add-info int-prim`, which goes through the compiler's SigOp descriptor
   (`Arith.SigOp.Builders`), its dispatcher (`SigOp.Info.semP`) and `Arith.Prim.add-semM`, and
   only then reaches `Word._⊕_`.
   Target: `Spec` defines `Word` (with `_⊕_` …), `TargetNum` and the primitive operations
   directly, and `primSem p-add n (a , b) = a ⊕ b`. The arith lowering imports them from the
   Spec. Same for `restrictᵛ`/`bindᵛ` (`Denotation.PhaseV`).
   PARTLY DONE (2026-10-13): `Spec.Core.Meaning.primSem` states each primitive directly over
   `Word.Width` and `Float.Arith` (no SigOp descriptor, no dispatcher); the Bool encoding is
   `Spec.Bool.truth`, which the compiler's `Arith.Prim.bool` reuses. Left: `Word`, `Float.Arith`
   and `TargetNum` move into the Spec (phase C), and `restrictᵛ`/`bindᵛ`. `Word` DONE
   (2026-10-13): its definitions are `Spec.Word`; `Once.Word` keeps the laws (`WidthLaws`) and the
   compiler's strength-reduction forms (`shlᵂ`, `sdiv2ᵏ`).
9. **The front-end relations were written by transcribing the lexer and parser.** This does not
   make the theorem vacuous: the relations are inhabited, and soundness and completeness are
   real proofs against them. But where the relation mentions the implementation, a defect in
   the implementation becomes the language, and no proof can see it. There are two kinds:
   * *Positive helpers* such as `headK`, `collectDigitsB`, `skipNewlines` and `allTrailing`.
     These are small total functions that *are* the lexical definition. They move into the
     Spec (R1) and are reviewed as Spec.
   * *A negative premise on the parser.* `Spec.Parsing`'s `pds-stop` ends the declaration list
     where `parseDeclB toks' ≡ nothing`, that is, where the compiler's parser fails.
     Together with `allTrailing rest ≡ true`, a parser bug that rejects a valid declaration
     removes that program from the language: the Spec agrees with the parser by construction.
     Target: stop where only trailing filler is left, stated without the parser.
   The transcription also carried over behaviour that the language should decide on purpose.
   Both cases below need a decision entry:
   * `lex-string-err`: an unterminated string literal ends the token stream, and everything
     after the quote is dropped.
   * `lex-skip`: a character that starts no token is silently skipped.
10. **Lemmas in the Spec** (R6). There are about 50, in three groups, and each one is
    classified before it is moved (by who uses it, not by its name: `⊢close` reads like
    metatheory but `Spec.Elaboration` needs it):
    * *Needed by a definition.* Most exist because `Spec.Elaboration` is defined on typing
      *derivations* (a surface derivation maps to a core derivation, and the core meaning `⟦_⟧`
      is one clause per rule). Building those needs admissible rules: `⊢idᶜ`, `⊢var′`, `retype`,
      `ren-cong`, `absTy-⟦⟧`, `primDom-abs`, `⊢close`. `sigOpRefᵛ` needs `value-∈`/`base-contract` to find
      the interpretation's answer. These stay, gathered in a module that says what they are
      (the admissible rules the elaboration uses). The alternative is elaboration on terms plus
      a preservation metatheorem. But then the meaning would depend on which derivation the
      proof picks, which needs coherence. That is a design decision, not a cleanup.
    * *Properties used only by proofs outside the Spec* (e.g. `ModuleLaws.teleSig≡entrySig`,
      used by three `Adequacy` bridges; `thin-var-refl`, used by `Adequacy.CoreInst` and
      `Metatheory.CoreRenameSem`; `ModuleLaws` moved to `Once/Adequacy` 2026-10-13 — it imports
      the compiler, so not `Once/Metatheory`). These are not part of the
      definition, and not promises either. They move to `Once/Metatheory` (Spec-only imports,
      `metatheory-gate`) as a lemma library, unstated in `Spec.Metatheory`.
    * *Claims about the language* (e.g. `single-ty`: substituting a pure term is typed, used
      only by `Metatheory.TermModel`). These are metatheory: the statement goes
      in `Spec.Metatheory`, the proof under `Once/Metatheory`.
    `PolyTyping` importing `Metatheory.SubLaws` (for `<:-refl`) is the first group done wrong:
    either the definition stops needing it, or it becomes an admissible-rule definition in the
    Spec.

## Review findings (collected while moving; for phase F)

Things a reader of the Spec would trip over, found while moving definitions. None is fixed by
a move (a move must not change a definition); each needs its own step, and some need a decision.
* **Two contexts kept in parallel.** The judgment's context (`NamedCtx`) holds a named list
  (`TypeCheck.Context`) and a de Bruijn context (`Spec.Context.Ctx`) side by side, with nothing
  tying them together; lookups walk both in lock-step. One context with names is what a reader
  expects.
* **Two usage orders.** `_≤q_` (Boolean, `Spec.Type.Grade`) and `_≤q'_` (a relation,
  `Spec.Context`) are the same order. One relation is enough; the Boolean is a decider.
* **`RAna` is in the written syntax but the parser never produces it.** Either it is
  surface syntax or it is not part of what a programmer writes.
* **Polymorphic definitions in the context carry their bodies** (`PolyCtx` holds `RawExpr`).
  The judgment never reads the body (`t-var-poly-*` has "no body premise": a definition is
  typed once, at its declaration); it only carries it. The Spec's context should hold schemas.
* **Done along the way:** the elaborator's fresh-variable counter (`freshCounter`) was dead
  state in the judgment's context: nothing incremented it. It is removed.

## Phases

**A — The gate (done).** `make spec-gate` (in `make merge-gate`) fails on any finding not in
`spec-gate.baseline`, and on a baseline line that no longer applies. It finds four things:
- a module outside `Once/Spec` in the import closure (R1);
- an implementation re-export;
- a re-export without `using`;
- `public`/`renaming`/`hiding` in a Spec module (R2, R3).

**B — Top-down, starting at `Once.Spec`.** One module at a time, from the root down the import
tree. Apply R1–R4, R9 and R10 to the module, then follow the red into its importers and the
modules it now has to import directly. Each step is small and leaves the tree green.
1. `Once.Spec` becomes the index: it imports every Spec module and exports nothing.
   `Once.Certified` imports `CorrectCompiler` from `Spec.Correct` and `Metatheory` from
   `Spec.Metatheory`. `spec-closure.py`'s roots become every module under `Once/Spec`.
2. Its children, in the index's order: `Spec.Type`, `Spec.Syntax`, `Spec.Typing`, `Spec.Meaning`,
   `Spec.Correct`, `Spec.Program`, `Spec.Contract`, `Spec.Metatheory`. A child that is only a
   re-export shell (`Type`, `Syntax`, `Typing`, `Meaning`, `Correct`) cannot be fixed by deleting
   its `public`: its definitions live in the implementation. For these, step 2 is phase C applied
   to that one module.
3. Their children, down to the leaves.

**C — Move the definitions in, reverse the imports.** For each implementation module the Spec
needs:
- its definitions move to their place in the target structure;
- the rest stays where it is (deciders, `show`, error reporting);
- the implementation imports the Spec.

No shells are left: the old module either keeps implementation content or is deleted. Importers
are rewritten by an exact-token script (memory: bulk scripts, no tidy). Splitting `Once.Type`
(232 importers) is the largest step. C is driven by B: a module moves when the top-down walk
reaches it.

**D — Cut the borrowings (R7).** Items 1–10, each where the walk reaches it. Item 1 is the large
one (coordinate with plan 0.59). Items 3, 4 and 7 are small. Item 6 needs a decision entry.

**E — `--safe` (R5).** When a module's imports are all Spec modules or the library, it gets
`{-# OPTIONS --safe #-}`. Bottom-up, as leaves become clean.

**F — Review (§ Review).** Read the Spec as a whole against the target structure.

**G — Metatheory joins.** Coherence (`Adequacy.Coherence*`) and the meaning's laws
(`Denotation.{TraceMonad, GradedDomain, Behavior}Laws`) are stated in `Spec.Metatheory` and
proved under `Once/Metatheory`, which the gate then allows.

Done when: `spec-gate.baseline` is empty, every Spec module is `--safe`, and the review passes.

## Review

The changes to the Spec are reviewed as the language definition, not as a refactoring. Before
each merge of this plan's work, and once over the whole Spec at the end:

* **What it says.** For every definition that moved or changed: is it what a Once program means
  (or may be)? A move must not change a definition; a change gets a decision entry. Compare the
  normalized statements before and after where it is unclear.
* **What a categorical semanticist expects.**
  - The objects are the types, and the grading is visible.
  - Σ is a signature of generators.
  - The core is a calculus whose syntactic category is the free structure on Σ: substitution
    is composition, and the derived combinators are the categorical operations.
  - A model is an interpretation of Σ in sets with the monad `T`, and `⟦_⟧` is the induced
    interpretation.
  - `μ`/`ν` are the initial algebra and final coalgebra.
  - The observation is the trace.
  - Anything the Spec states that this reader would find foreign (a machine word, a register, a
    parser state, a fuel parameter) is either justified in its header or moved out.
* **The rules.** R1–R10, module by module.
* **The gates.** `spec-gate`, `metatheory-gate`, `make certified`, the backstop, and Agda's
  `--safe`.
