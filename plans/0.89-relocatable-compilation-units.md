# Plan 0.89 — Relocatable compilation units

**Decision:** D159. **Branch:** `plan-0.89-relocatable-units`.
**Status:** roadmap (2026-09-07).

The compiler shall output RELOCATABLE code, and that shall be encoded in the
emitter's codomain (OCP-0005 style: the violation is unsayable) rather than
asserted as a premise. See D159 for the argument and D157/D158 for what forced
it.

**Method, and it is not negotiable:** top-down. Make the top-level change, let
the typechecker produce the obligations, and fix what goes red. Do NOT build
supporting lemmas in advance — D154 is the standing lesson: a lemma whose shape
was derived by reasoning about a proof you have not written is a guess.

---

## 0. Where we are

`comp-value-realized` is proved (D158) but through `SpanAt prog base` — a
threaded placement premise, which is the weak encoding. `obs-correct-curry` and
`obs-correct-apply` remain axioms because `curry` inlines its body into its own
trace, so no single placement maps both it and `apply` (D157).

`IRObsCorrectFlat`: 17 named residuals. Apex green on
`plan-0.83-0.80-0.59-spec-independence` at `4f1e1bdb`.

**The channel already exists at three levels and nothing fills it** —
`ir-to-trace'`'s fourth component, `ir-to-bodies{,-from}`, and
`emit-thunk-body` in all three backends. Phase A is mostly REVIVAL.

## Phase A — the encoding (the type change)

The whole point. Everything else is consequence.

* **A1.** `CompUnit` in `Once.CCC.Codegen.IRToTrace`:
  `entry : AbstractTrace` (block-local positions only) and
  `blocks : List (LabelId × ℕ × AbstractTrace)` — the shape the fourth
  component and `emit-thunk-body` already use: (label, frame budget, trace).
* **A2.** `ir-to-unit : IR A B → CompUnit`, `link : CompUnit → AbstractTrace`,
  and `ir-to-trace ir = link (ir-to-unit ir)` so the APEX STATEMENT IS
  UNCHANGED and the arch pipeline keeps consuming a linked image.
* **A3.** `curry` emits its body as a BLOCK instead of inlining it. This deletes
  `c-jmp (ℓ o end-label)` and `end-label` — they exist only to jump over the
  inlined body — halving `curry`'s label usage.
* **A4.** `Cata` likewise: its algebra becomes a block. Its own comment already
  says the algebra "runs in its OWN frame as a called body (exactly as `curry`'s
  body is generated)"; today `cata-dispatch` splices it.
* **A5.** Composition of units is UNION of block maps. State it; do not yet
  prove anything about it.

**Exit:** `ir-to-trace` unchanged extensionally where it matters; the emitted
asm gains real `.L_thunk_` blocks (the backends' dead path comes alive).

### A2 CANNOT BE A NO-OP STEP — measured 2026-09-07

Tried A1+A2 alone, expecting a behaviour-preserving refactor: `link-onto` written
as an accumulator so `link (unit e []) ≡ e` definitionally, hence
`ir-to-trace ir` still reducing through `proj-trace`. It does not. `link-onto e bs`
reduces only when `bs` is LITERALLY `[]`, and `proj-bodies (ir-to-trace' 0 0 ir)`
is stuck on the variable `ir`. First red, immediately:

    FrameFreeTrace.agda:342  ir-to-trace-frame-free : FrameFreeTrace (ir-to-trace ir)
    …proved by `frame-free-at-frontier ir hm 0`, i.e. about `proj-trace (…)`.

**And that is the point, not an obstacle.** Every whole-trace invariant in the
codebase is currently proved about the ENTRY BLOCK and silently equated with
"the program". `link` forces each to say what it was always supposed to say.
The six:

  * `Straight (ir-to-trace ir)`                        — StraightTrace:170
  * `FrameFreeTrace (ir-to-trace ir)`                  — FrameFreeTrace:342
  * `EmittableI` at every fetchable index              — FrameFreeTrace:354
  * label scoping / `seg-at` agreement                 — LabelScope:1666-1671
  * `AllSlotStable (ir-to-trace ir)`                   — CataIRSlotStable:386
  * `SegOK (ir-stack-budget ir) (ir-to-trace ir)`      — SlotBudget:1162

Each needs a `link` preservation lemma: `P (entry u) → All (P ∘ layout) (blocks u)
→ P (link u)`. Today the second premise is vacuous but NOT definitionally so;
under A3 it becomes real content ("the bodies are frame-free too", …).

`SegOK` is the one to look at hardest, and it is a genuine semantic gain: it
applies `ir-stack-budget ir` — the ENTRY block's budget — to the whole program.
For a multi-block program that is simply wrong; each block has its own budget
(the `ℕ` in the triple, which is exactly what `emit-thunk-body` turns into
`subq body-budget*8`). Units force the invariant to become per-block, matching
what the backends already do.

**Consequence for sequencing:** A2 and A3 land TOGETHER (a unit is only
meaningful once something fills it), with the six preservation lemmas in the
same step. Do not attempt A2 as a separate green commit — it cannot be one.
`ir-to-trace` and `ir-to-trace-at-frontier 0` also stop being definitionally
equal, so route both through `link` at the same time.

### The entry block needs a TERMINATOR, and the abstract model has none

Writing `link` surfaced it. The concrete layout per top-level function
(`Compile.agda:537-541`) is

    functionPrologue ++ [ subq / trace / addq ] ++ functionEpilogue ++ bodies

and `functionEpilogue = "    ret\n\n"` on every target. The ABSTRACT trace has
no representation of that `ret`: it ends by falling off the end, and the obs
layer's `at-end` (D155) says exactly that — the run finishes at
`fpc ≡ length (emitted …)`.

That is correct only while nothing follows the entry. Fill the bodies channel
and the entry runs straight into the first thunk body — abstractly AND
concretely. So `link` must place the terminator, which finally makes the
abstract model represent the epilogue it currently elides, and gives `at-end`
a real instruction to name instead of an absence.

Consequence for A1: the unit carries the entry's frame budget, because the
terminator is `c-ret budget`:

    record CompUnit : Set where
      entry-budget : ℕ
      entry        : AbstractTrace
      blocks       : List (ℕ × ℕ × AbstractTrace)

which is precisely components 1, 3 and 4 of `ir-to-trace'`'s existing 4-tuple —
component 2 being the label counter that is threading state, not output. So
`ir-to-trace' n l ir` already IS `(label-counter , CompUnit)`, and A1 is close to
a re-bracketing of what the emitter returns.

### The three findings are one finding

All three are invisible while `bodies ≡ []`, and all three become real the
moment it is filled:

  1. the six whole-trace invariants are proved about the ENTRY BLOCK;
  2. `SegOK` applies the ENTRY BLOCK's budget to the whole program;
  3. the abstract trace has no epilogue, so it ends by FALLING OFF the entry
     block.

Each is the same mistake: **the abstract model describes the main block and
calls it the program.** That is the semantic/structural defect D159 is really
about, and the empty bodies channel is what has kept it hidden.

## Phase B finding: the thunk symbol does not match itself (all three targets)

Checking what `BlockRef` should be keyed by turned up a real defect in the
emitted assembly, latent behind the empty bodies channel.

The two sides of a closure-body symbol are rendered differently:

    reference (code-address load / jump)
      X86-64/Emit:  showMem (rip+label n) = ".L_thunk_" ++ showLabelId n ++ "(%rip)"
      X86-32/Emit:  showInstr (mov-code r n) = "movl $.L_thunk_" ++ showLabelId n
      RiscV64/Emit: showInstr (lla rd n) = "lla … .L_thunk_" ++ showLabelId n

    definition (the block itself), all three in Target/<arch>.agda
      emit-thunk-body … (lbl , budget , t) = ".L_thunk_" ++ showNat lbl ++ ":"

`showLabelId lid = once-symbol-path (owner lid) ++ showPath (path lid) ++ "_"
++ showNat (idx lid)` — so a reference renders `.L_thunk_once_4main_10` while
the definition renders `.L_thunk_10`. They are different symbols.

This is invisible today ONLY because `ir-to-bodies` is always `[]`, so
`emit-thunk-body` never runs. The moment the bodies channel is filled — which is
exactly what Phase A does — every closure body is DEFINED under a name nothing
REFERENCES, on all three targets. That is an assembler error at best.

(`Compile.agda:582` records a past incident with the real symbol shape,
`.L_thunk_once_4main_10`, confirming which side is the intended one.)

**This settles `BlockRef`'s key.** `CompUnit.blocks` must be keyed by `LabelId`,
not by a raw `ℕ`. The reference side already is; the definition side is the one
that is wrong, and the raw `ℕ` is what let the two drift apart. Keying the block
by its `LabelId` from the start is the project's own "canonicalise as early as
possible" rule — the block's identity should not be a bare counter that only
becomes a symbol once `ℓ o` is applied at one of the two sites, differently.

**Fourth instance of the D159 pattern**, and the first that is a defect in the
OUTPUT rather than in a proof: per-block budgets, the missing epilogue, the
entry-vs-program invariants, and now a symbol that does not match itself. All
four were hidden by the same empty channel.


## Phase B REFINED (2026-09-08): the PROGRAM becomes a unit; the PC stays a number

B1 as written says `fpc` becomes `LabelId × ℕ`. Measurement says that is the
wrong move, and there is a better one that gets the whole benefit.

WHAT THE MEASUREMENT SAYS. `exec-flat` has only 60 call sites outside
`Machine.Flat`, and only THREE in the arch layer (X86-64/FlatSimulation,
X86-32/FlatSimulation, FlatCore/FlatCorrespondence). The 922 `fpc` figure is
almost entirely PROJECTIONS — `fpc fs` read out of a `FlatState` in the arch
correspondence — and those are what a change to `fpc`'s TYPE would touch.

So the fork looked like:
  (A) two machines — `exec-unit` over units for the obs layer, `exec-flat` over
      lists for the arch layer, joined by `link-correct`;
  (B) one machine, retype `fpc`, ~880 projection sites through the arch layer
      including riscv64's call window.

(A) is how `exec-trace` vs `exec-flat` happened, and D151 records how that ended:
proofs in one vocabulary that do not count in the other. A bridge does not save
it — StraightTrace and FlatSemanticLift WERE that bridge, and they are the
modules just deleted and left unwired. (B) keeps one semantics but is the sweep.

NEITHER IS NEEDED. The benefit wanted from Phase B is that `find-label` be
BLOCK-LOCAL and `find-thunk` be a lookup instead of a scan. That needs the
machine to know where the block boundaries ARE — i.e. the PROGRAM must be a
`CompUnit`. It does NOT need the pc to be symbolic:

    exec-flat : ℕ → CompUnit → FlatState → FlatState     -- program: a unit
    fpc       : ℕ                                        -- unchanged
    find-label u pc m  — scans only the block containing `pc`
    find-thunk u ℓ     — looks `ℓ` up in `blocks u`, no instruction scan

The pc stays an index into the linked image, so `blk-off`, `pc-off`, `ret-eq`
and all ~880 projections are untouched; the arch layer's three `exec-flat` calls
pass a unit instead of a list. One semantics, no bridge, no sweep.

THE LESSON, and it is the same one twice now: the thing that had to change was
the PROGRAM's type, not the POSITION's. D159 said a list is a placement; the
correction here is that making positions symbolic was never what that implied —
knowing the block STRUCTURE is. Symbolic pcs remain available later (Phase F) if
`blk-off` ever needs to collapse into `caddr + local`, but nothing forces it now.


## Phase B step 4 — the payoff, specified from the code

Read off `Machine.Flat` as it stands. `fpc` stays `ℕ` (an index into `link u`);
only the PROGRAM's type changes, per the refinement above.

    exec-flat : ℕ → CompUnit → FlatState → FlatState

**`find-thunk` stops being a scan.** Today it is `ft-go`, walking the whole
instruction list for a `c-thunk` marker. A thunk label IS a block name, so it
becomes a lookup over `blocks u` with an offset accumulator:

    blocks-start : CompUnit → ℕ
    blocks-start u = suc (length (entry u))        -- entry, then its `c-ret`

    ft-blocks : List (LabelId × ℕ × AbstractTrace) → LabelId → ℕ → Maybe ℕ
    ft-blocks []                 _      _   = nothing
    ft-blocks ((l , b , t) ∷ bs) target off =
      ft-blk (l ≡ᵇᴵ target) bs target off (length (block-layout (l , b , t)))
    ft-blk true  _  _      off _   = just off
    ft-blk false bs target off len = ft-blocks bs target (off + len)

    find-thunk : CompUnit → LabelId → Maybe ℕ
    find-thunk u target = ft-blocks (blocks u) target (blocks-start u)

`thunk-of?`, `ft-go`, `ft-at`, `ft-match` and `ft-go-sound` all go with it —
`ft-go-sound` existed to say "what the scan found IS a body entry for that
label", which a lookup gives by construction. Same for `ft-go-++-miss`.

**`find-label` becomes block-local.** It takes the pc so it can restrict to the
block containing it:

    block-at   : CompUnit → ℕ → Maybe (ℕ × AbstractTrace)   -- (start , block)
    find-label : CompUnit → ℕ → LabelId → Maybe ℕ
    find-label u pc target with block-at u pc
    … | just (start , blk) = mmap (start +_) (fl-go blk target 0)
    … | nothing            = nothing

`fl-go` survives unchanged as the WITHIN-BLOCK scan. What dies is the ability to
resolve a label in another block — which is exactly the fact `NoCross` was going
to have to prove, now unsayable.

**What this is expected to delete, and the point of the phase:**
  * `segagree-++ⁿ` and the `NoCross` companions — the question evaporates.
  * most of LabelScope's eleven bespoke combinators, for the same reason.
  * `Shifted` + the twelve `shifted-*` + `exec-flat-reloc` — already unused
    since D158, deletable once nothing else references them.

**Call sites:** 60 `exec-flat` outside `Machine.Flat`, of which 43 are the
codegen layer (FlatStepLemmas 18, IRObsCorrectFlat 15, CataNextSlot 10) and
THREE the arch layer. `fpc` projections are untouched.

**`traces-agree` lands here too.** With the program a unit and `link` below the
machine, its statement can be program-indexed the same way `value-realized`
already is, bounded by the chain (`chain-events`), rather than by an `∃ fuel`
that over-collects the successor's events.


## MEASUREMENT CORRECTION (2026-09-08): Phase B step 4 is ~1700 sites, not 60

The refinement above sized Phase B from `exec-flat`'s call sites: 60 outside
`Machine.Flat`, only three in the arch layer, therefore "one semantics, no
bridge, no sweep". That was the wrong function to measure.

The arch layer barely calls `exec-flat`. It uses the PER-INSTRUCTION step and
the fetch:

    flat-exec-instr, outside Machine.Flat        432   (369 in Adequacy)
      X86-64/FlatSimulation      78
      X86-32/FlatSimulation      78
      FlatCore/EventDispatch     78
      RiscV64/FlatSimulation     68
      FlatCore/FlatCorrespondence 39
      FlatCore/RunWF             28
    fetch, outside Machine.Flat                 1462   (1271 in Adequacy)

`flat-exec-instr` is what calls `do-jump` / `do-branch` / `do-call`, so making
label resolution unit-aware means IT takes the unit, and that sweeps the whole
arch correspondence — the three landed FlatSimulations, the 1661-line FlatCore
engine, and riscv64's call window (D105–D107).

**This inverts the re-sequencing.** LabelScope's remaining work was moved into
Phase B on the argument that its companions would be thrown away by a cheap
structural fix. The structural fix is not cheap:

    prove it     — `NoCross` companions, ~58 clauses over two inductions,
                   most of them vacuous (most shapes emit no blocks)
    make it structural — ~1700 sites through the riskiest landed code

So the honest ordering is the reverse of what I proposed: finish Phase A with
the companions, and treat block-local `find-label` as a later, separately
justified change — one whose payoff is no longer "it deletes the companions"
(they would already exist) but "it deletes eleven bespoke combinators and makes
the property unsayable".

**Method note, and it is the same error twice.** I sized a change from one
function's call sites and generalised. Earlier in this phase I classified a
module as unwired from a build that stopped early. Both times the fix was the
same: measure the thing that actually changes, not a proxy for it — here,
`flat-exec-instr` and `fetch`, not `exec-flat`.

## PHASE A CLOSED (2026-09-08): LabelScope covers the linked program (D160)

The last red module from D159's `link` change is green, with no new residual.

`emitted-jump-in-segment` used to discharge a goal about `ir-to-trace ir` — the
LINKED program, blocks and all — with `seg-agree ir 0 0`, which is `SegAgree` of
the ENTRY BLOCK. That was one of the four defects the always-empty `bodies`
channel had been hiding: with `ir-to-bodies ir ≡ []` for every `ir`, the entry
WAS the program, so the mismatch could not be observed.

**The route taken was the `NoCross` companions, not block-local `find-label`.**
The sizing that settled it stands: `find-label` block-locality is ~1700 sites
through three landed `FlatSimulation`s, the FlatCore engine and riscv64's call
window; the companions are one record through one 29-clause walk. And `NoCross`
is not a shim — it is the fact that is actually true, with disjoint windows
being merely one way to supply it (D159 said as much; D160 acts on it).

Landed in `Once/CCC/Codegen/LabelScope.agda` (+557 / −39):

* `NoCross` combinator layer — `nocross-{nolabˡ,nolabʳ,win,++ˡ,++ʳ,nil-l,nil-r}`.
  The two `++` laws are the point: `SegAgree` is not closed under `++`,
  `NoCross` is.
* `CataSplit` — the cata skeleton decomposition, extracted from the four
  `cata-*-agree` `where`-blocks and made their RESULT. `cata-agree` is now one
  application of `split-agree`; `split-nc-l` / `split-nc-r` are the block side,
  each one application of the same split.
* `ScopeOK` + `scope-ok` — the 29-clause walk carrying block-window, block
  `SegAgree`, and both `NoCross` directions together.
* `linked-agree : ∀ ir → SegAgree (ir-to-trace ir)` — the splice, via
  `segagree-++ⁿ`, consumed by `emitted-jump-in-segment`.

### What this pins for later phases

The composites' `NoCross` comes from window disjointness ACROSS the two
sub-IRs. That is the surviving load-bearing use of the label windows: if a
later phase renumbers labels per block (Phase F territory), `scope-ok`'s
composite cases are what will need re-proving, and `nocross-win` is the only
place they touch the counter.
