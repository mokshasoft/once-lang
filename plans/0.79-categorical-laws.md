# Plan 0.79 — What the Categorical Laws Are FOR

**Status:** plan (2026-09-01), not started. **Relates:** D063, D127, D130, D131.
**Premise:** there are TWO families of law and the repo currently has neither
in a useful place.

---

## 0. The finding that prompted this

`Once.Category.Laws` proves the CCC laws — identity, associativity, the
product/coproduct/exponential universal properties, `cata ∘ In` — and it is
unreachable from every gate root. Worse than unwired:

  * It has been imported EXACTLY ONCE in the repo's history, by the
    2025-12-13 "verified optimizer" commit, which used the laws as rewrite
    rules. That optimizer's correctness module is itself an island now, and
    **D039 found the optimizer unsound.** Its only consumer is dead.
  * It is stated over `Once.CCC.Eval.eval` — the PURE value evaluator — and
    `Denotation.DenotTrace` explicitly disowns that model: "a second value
    model that diverged from the trace for EFFECTFUL algebras — the same
    category-error as the retired ℤ proof-model."

So the laws are currently proved about a model the codebase has rejected.

## 1. The two families, which must not be conflated

**A. SOURCE-LEVEL laws — metatheory of the SPEC, user-facing.**

    ⟦ compose f (compose g h) ⟧ᶜ  ≡  ⟦ compose (compose f g) h ⟧ᶜ
    ⟦ compose id f ⟧ᶜ             ≡  ⟦ f ⟧ᶜ
    ⟦ fst (pair f g) ⟧ᶜ           ≡  ⟦ f ⟧ᶜ

These tell a PROGRAMMER that a refactoring is meaning-preserving. Stated over
the spec's own denotation, they are Spec metatheory and belong with it — not
over any IR, so they survive a change of IR.

**B. IR-LEVEL laws — metatheory of the implementation.**

The same equations over `evalᴰ` (the trace semantics, the ONE model). These
justify an OPTIMIZER: a rewrite `id ∘ f ⇝ f` is sound because of them. They
have no user-facing content.

`Category.Laws` is neither: IR-level, but over the disowned `eval`.

## 2. The decisions this plan owes

1. **Do the source-level laws become a stated part of the language?** If yes
   they are Spec metatheory and go beside `Once.Spec.Meaning`, and the README
   / language docs can quote them. If no, say so — "Once does not promise
   these equations" is a legitimate answer, and better than an unwired module
   implying it does.
2. **Is `Category.Laws` restated over `evalᴰ`, or deleted?** Deleting is
   defensible: its consumer was an unsound optimizer, and nothing else has
   ever needed it. Restating is only worth it if a verified optimizer is
   actually planned.
3. **How hard are they, really?** Cheap, and that is itself the finding:
   `⟦_⟧ᶜ` is compositional into the trace monad, so associativity of
   composition falls out of associativity of `>>=T`. They are near-
   definitional. That is WHY nothing broke from their being unwired, and why
   "wire them" is not by itself a goal — a law nothing consumes is
   documentation, and should be honest about being documentation.

## 3. What is NOT in scope

`Once.Semantics.Value.Laws` stays as it is. It is SEMANTICS, not laws about
the language: `sem-cata-In-id`, `sem-CoIn-CoOut`, `sem-ana-Out-id` are the
initial/final-algebra identities of the value CARRIERS. It was split out of
`Semantics.Value` deliberately (plan 0.47 step 3) so the denotation could
import the semantic functions without dragging in `funext` and
`bisimS-to-eq` — keeping those axioms OUT of the reachable cone is the point,
so its island status is by design. (`Semantics/Functor/Laws` IS reachable; the
functor-level laws are wired.)

## 4. The O2 correction this plan must carry

Plan 0.76 owes O2, phrased as: `⊢ᵐ`'s structural recursion "forces the
categorical LAWS through the agreement bridge", so their forcing must be
re-established after D127 deletes the realm.

**That premise does not survive contact with the evidence.** The laws module
was never imported by the bridge, in any commit, and the bridge (`faithful`,
`realize-agrees`) proves every case COMPUTATIONALLY. Nothing was forcing the
laws, because nothing consumed them.

So O2 is not "where are the laws forced now" — it is **"what did `⊢ᵐ`
actually buy?"**, answered case by case. One answer is already known and
recorded (D131): `CataFold.cata-fold-eq` takes the algebra's purity as a
hypothesis, discharged by `extract-morph-eff-denotes` — i.e. by the realm.
That is a REAL thing the realm was supplying, and the parameterized cata is
where it is supplied instead. Whoever closes 0.76 should write O2 that way and
amend the plan's text, rather than answer the question as posed.
