# Plan 0.79 — The language states its categorical laws

## Summary

Once promises that programs can be reasoned about like mathematics: you can replace one piece of a
program by another that is equal by a known law, and the program means the same. Today the language
writes down only some of those laws (folds, unfolds, effects). The basic ones, such as "taking the first
half of a pair gives back what you put in" or "choosing on a left value takes the left branch", are not
stated anywhere. A module that proved them about an old, abandoned model was deleted, and nothing took
its place. This plan states those laws as part of the language definition and proves them, with no
axioms, so a programmer (and the compiler's own optimizer) can rely on them.

## 1. Why

GOAL.md makes the laws a promise: reasoning about a Once program is "ordinary mathematics: equational
reasoning, naturality, fold fusion, the laws of the effect structure", and a categorical mathematician
reads the Spec as "a cartesian closed core, initial algebras and final coalgebras, effects as a Freyd
category". A language that promises equational reasoning must state the equations. They are properties
of the language, so they are Spec metatheory (`Once.Spec.Metatheory`, statements; proofs under
`Once/Metatheory`; carried by `CertifiedBuild.language`).

Three consequences of the project's principles fix their form:
- **Over the Spec's own meaning**, never over an implementation model. `Category.Laws` was stated over the
  pure `eval`, a model the codebase had rejected. Deleting that module (D347) was right; dropping its
  *claim* was not — no decision says the language stopped promising β/η.
- **No axiom**: equality is the Spec's observation (`SameM`), never `≡` with `funext` or `bisimS-to-eq`.
  The old internal copies (`Semantics.Value.Laws`) are the wrong form for this reason too.
- **Effects are honest**: a law that would drop, duplicate or reorder an effect holds only for
  effect-free terms, and says so.

The IR-level laws (the same equations over the implementation's trace semantics) are NOT owed: the
optimizer is proved trace-preserving directly (`Adequacy.OptimizePreserves`, D347). They were a means,
not a promise.

## 2. Where we are (2026-11-02)

The core is a λ-calculus (`Once.Spec.Core.Syntax.Tm`); the categorical combinators are derived λ-terms
(`Once.Spec.Core.Derived`: `composeᶜ`, `pairᶜ`, `caseᶜ`, `curryᶜ`, …). So the generating laws are the
core's equational theory (a graded computational λ-calculus, in Moggi's sense), and the combinator laws
are its corollaries.

| Structure (GOAL) | Laws it owes | In the Spec's metatheory now |
|---|---|---|
| Category | `compose` identity, associativity | missing (corollary of → and `let` laws) |
| Products | β₁, β₂, η for effect-free arms | `Freyd.pair-β₁/β₂/η`, over the effect domain's `pairK`, not over terms |
| Coproducts | β (any scrutinee), η, `case` commutes with `let` | missing |
| Terminal | `Unit` terminal for effect-free terms | missing |
| Initial | nothing runs after a `Void` | missing |
| Exponentials | β (call-by-value), η for effect-free functions | `TermModel.let-β` only |
| Effect structure (`let` = bind) | unit, associativity, effect-free discard | `Effects` (domain level); `let-β` (left unit, pure) |
| Initial algebras | computation, uniqueness, so identity and fusion | `Recursion.fold-in/fold-unique/fold-fusion` (domain level) ✓ |
| Final coalgebras | computation, uniqueness | `Recursion.unfold-out/unfold-unique` (domain level) ✓ |
| Naturality | a polymorphic definition is natural | missing |

The drafted statements (§7) cover the λ-calculus rows. They type-check under `--safe` against the
Spec as an untracked scratch module (`formal/LawsDraft.agda`).

## 3. Decisions

- **D1, which laws are stated — DECIDED (user, 2026-11-02): the category theorist's view.** The
  structure is stated first, by its universal properties on the arrows a program writes (`Categorical`):
  the arrows at every grade form a category with coproducts and an initial object (the Kleisli side of
  the Freyd category), and the effect-free arrows form a bicartesian closed category. β and η together
  are each universal property. The λ-calculus laws follow as its internal language (`Equations`: the
  Lambek–Scott correspondence for the pure core, Moggi's computational λ-calculus for `let` and effects),
  and are stated too. The metatheory does not define the language, so the number of statements is no
  argument either way.
- **D2, the form of equality.** Drafted: two derivations of one judgment (same context, usage, type and
  grade) are equal when their meanings are `SameM` at every instance, environment and definitions'
  meaning — the form `TermModel.let-β` already uses. Each law takes both sides' derivations as
  hypotheses, so no usage arithmetic is stated. Recommended as drafted.
- **D3, the effect side condition.** Drafted: `Pure` = the term has a derivation at grade `pure` (a
  syntactic, checkable condition). Alternative: semantic centrality (`Freyd.Central`). Recommended:
  `Pure` (D250: pure means referentially transparent).
- **D4, term-level fold/unfold laws.** The domain-level `Recursion` laws exist; term-level ones
  (`fold F alg (roll F t) ≈ app alg (map (fold …) t)`) need a term-level functor action `mapᶜ` in
  `Core.Derived`, which the Spec lacks. Add it, or rely on the domain level. Recommended: add `mapᶜ`
  (a programmer reasons about terms).
- **D5, naturality.** Stating "a polymorphic definition is natural" needs a relational reading of types
  (the Spec's `Same` relates a type to itself only). Recommended: phase 2, starting with definitions of
  type `F a → G a` against effect-free maps.
