# Plan 0.153 — Every proof in the tree checks without `with`, and no module is slow to check

## Summary

The proofs are checked by a program that has to re-check a module and everything built on it after
every change. One common shorthand in those proofs makes the checker do far more work than needed: on
one module it turned a one-minute check into one that ran out of memory. This plan rewrites every use
of that shorthand in the tree into the cheaper form that says the same thing, slowest modules first,
and keeps a gate so it does not come back. When it is done, nothing in the tree uses it, and every
module checks within a fixed time.

## 1. Why

A `with` abstracts the goal over its scrutinee, and the type checker re-checks the abstracted goal; over
large goals (the linear core's configurations, the arch simulations) that costs gigabytes and minutes per
module. `rewrite` is a `with` in disguise. A slow module slows every check of every module above it, every
`make certified`, every agent's fix loop, and two sessions share one 7.6 GB machine with a 3.3 GB cap per
process (plan 0.152). Measured on 2026-11-20 (plan 0.146, B1.7b phase 2): `LinCore.LowerSim` blew a 2.7 GB
heap after 2.5 minutes; with its ten `with`s rewritten as `case … of λ where` and typed continuations it
checks in 57 s at 1.4 GB.

## 2. Where we are

- `formal/scripts/with-gate.sh` (added by plan 0.146) counts `with`/`rewrite` clauses in the closure of
  `Once/Certified.agda` + `Once/Extract/Root.agda`, against `scripts/with-gate.baseline`: a ratchet, a
  module's count may only go down, and a module not listed carries none. Part of `make merge-gate`.
- At 2026-11-20, before plan 0.146 removes its own: 834 modules in the closure, 3644 clauses in 313
  modules. The heaviest: `Grammar/ExprBridge` 278, `CCC/Machine/SMPrimitives` 202, `Parser/Expr` 156,
  `LinCore/Events` 101 (0.146's), `Grammar/Convert` 90, `Parser/Generic/Sound` 79,
  `Grammar/ParserBridge` 74, `Format/Proof/WF` 64, `Adequacy/LexerBridge` 54, `Arith/Backend/Correct` 53.
  Plan 0.146 takes out the 436 its branch added (41 modules, mostly `LinCore`) before it merges; this
  plan starts from the baseline as 0.146 leaves it.
- The count is a proxy. The cost is per module: `make certified-per-module` (one module per process)
  and `make profile` (wall time and peak memory per module) measure it.

## 3. Decisions

1. **Order: by measured cost, not by count.** A module with many cheap `with`s over small datatypes can
   be fast; one `with` over a large goal can be slow. Recommendation: per-module timings first, slowest
   first; the cheap ones go in batches. (Open.)
2. **The rewrite forms** (the user, 2026-11-13, decided): a result is destructured by a local function of
   explicit type (a typed continuation, `fin : Σ … → Goal`) or by `case e of λ where …`
   (`Function.case_of_`). A `with` that refines a decision used inside another definition becomes a
   helper over the `Dec` that definition unfolds to (as `LowerSim.size-with-same` over `size-with-aux`).
   `rewrite` becomes `subst`/`cong`/`trans`.
3. **A time cap per module.** Recommendation: 60 s and 3 GB on its own (KEEP-GOING.md's numbers),
   checked by a gate over `make profile`'s report once the tree meets it. (Open.)

## 4. Steps

1. Measure: `make certified-per-module` (or `make profile`) on master after 0.146 merges; list every
   module over 30 s or 2 GB with its `with` count.
2. Rewrite the slowest module's `with`s; check it alone (`make agda MODULE=…` under `/usr/bin/time -v`),
   then `scripts/agda-keepgoing.py Once/Certified.agda` for its dependants; lower its baseline line
   (`scripts/with-gate.sh --baseline`). Commit per module or per small batch.
3. Repeat down the timing list; then the cheap remainder in batches by directory.
4. When the baseline is empty, delete it (the gate then forbids any `with`), and add the time-cap gate
   (decision 3).

## 5. Done when

`scripts/with-gate.baseline` is empty and deleted; `make merge-gate` forbids any `with`/`rewrite` in the
apex and compiler closure; every module checks within the cap on its own; `make certified` is faster
than at the start, with the before/after numbers recorded here.
