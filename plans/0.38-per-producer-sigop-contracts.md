> statement; this plan is the implementation breakdown. Keep them reconciled.
>

> obligation `build-pure` was *false* under the `classify-name` string-match (see
> "Re-triggered" section below). **`build-pure` has since been retired** (the
> clean-semantics `cata`/`ana` closure-bridge removed it), so `faithful` is
> already postulate-free — M0.3 is moot. Milestone 0 (`0.38-core`) remains on the
> critical path for *honesty* (deleting the name-guess catch-all); M1–M2 remain

> **Progress:** M0.1 (the abstract `Once.SigOp.Interpretation`) is landed and
> typechecks. M0.2 (thread it + delete the catch-all + re-extract) is the
> remaining work.

> Originally drafted while diagnosing the Plan 0.36 effectful-cata bug (a SigOp
> minted for an internal function laundered its meaning through the postulated
> `generic-sem`). This plan removes the *class*: a SigOp may only exist with its
> contract discharged by its producer, and the genuine-external contracts live
> where their subject lives — next to the implementation.

## What a SigOp actually is (corrected understanding, 2026-06-09)

A `SigOp` is **just the morphism `A → B`** — nothing more. It is an escape from
**CCC structure**, NOT from soundness. It is defined by Once *because it carries
a contract its producer must follow*. It is used:
- **externally** (syscalls — `linux.exit`, a real `emit`): behavior Once cannot
  derive *internally*, so the contract is discharged by the interpretation —
  **postulated** for an unverified kernel (Linux), or **proven** for a verified
  one (seL4, connected to its refinement theorems). Discharge is per
  interpretation × target, NOT "external ⟹ axiom";
- **internally-but-non-CCC** (the arith compiler's `arith.block.<digest>`):
  Once-generated subroutines that leave CCC; the contract is **proven** (the
  arith compiler discharges emit-correspondence + `PreservesCCC`).

So the soundness unit is: **a SigOp is sound iff its producer discharged its
contract.** Internal producers prove it; external producers postulate it. The
Plan 0.36 bug was a producer (an extraction in the cata path) minting a SigOp
**without discharging the contract** — permitted only because the contract is
decoupled from construction and a generic catch-all supplies it.

## Two trust boundaries, one of them currently invisible

For an external SigOp like `linux.exit`:
1. **Agda-level contract** — `PreservesCCC` + semI/semM coherence. Today:
   postulated/discharged somewhere downstream (per-name, partly via the generic
   pure path).
2. **Implementation ⊨ semantics** — does the linked `once_exit` asm actually do
   what `semM` says? **Today this is nowhere** — `Syscalls.x86_64` is linked at
   the end; nothing in the proof connects it to `semM`. Not even a postulate —
   an unstated assumption outside the verified boundary.

Boundary #2 is the real gap: the trust sits *away from its subject* (the asm),
so it can't be audited.

## The design

**Co-locate the contract with the implementation, at (SigOp × target)
granularity.**

- The `.once` interface file (`Syscalls.once`) declares only signatures.
- Each target implementation (`Syscalls.x86_64`, `Syscalls.riscv64`, …) is the
  asm that must satisfy the contract.
- Therefore each (interface-file × target) gets a paired **Agda contract
  module** holding the per-SigOp contract postulates for that file's ops on that
  target — e.g. `Strata/Interpretations/Linux/Syscalls/X86-64/Contract.agda`
  postulating `once_exit ⊨ linux.exit-contract`, `once_write ⊨ …`, etc.
  (Granularity of the *obligation* is per SigOp × target — honest, since the
  trust is per-asm; the *module* is per interface-file × target, mirroring the
  `Syscalls.once` ↔ `Syscalls.<target>` pairing.)
- These postulates ARE the external trusted base — genuine axioms (you cannot
  prove a syscall). They feed Plan 0.11's `TrustedBase` as the *confined,
  per-producer* external axioms.

**Internal producers prove, never postulate.** Arith already does; a cata that
ever genuinely emits a SigOp (rare — its algebra is normally CCC code) would
have to prove its contract too. No internal producer touches the external
contract modules.

**Eliminate the generic catch-all.** `generic-info : String → SigOpInfo A B`
(materialising postulated `generic-sem` for *any* name) is the laundering
vector — it lets a producer mint a SigOp for a name with no real contract. A
SigOpInfo should only be constructible **with** its contract (from the
producer's contract module / a proof), so a contractless SigOp is a type error
(this is Plan 0.11's "obligation local to construction"). The opaque external
semantics survive only behind a **genuine-external token** that a Strata
signature + its contract module can mint and internal code cannot forge.

## Relationship to 0.11

0.11 parameterizes the trusted base and bans `postulate` outside `TrustedBase`.
0.38 says *what the SigOp entries of that base are*: not one `generic-sem`
catch-all, but a set of per-(SigOp × target) implementation-contract axioms,
co-located with their impls and surfaced into `TrustedBase`. 0.11 makes them
visible; 0.38 makes them honest (per-asm) and unforgeable (no catch-all).

## Three distinct times — and why the core is interpretation-AGNOSTIC (2026-06-17, refined 2026-06-20)

Interpretation contracts touch **none** of the moments where Once code is built
or run as a *compiler*. There are three separate times, and only one of them
involves an interpretation at all:

1. **Compiler-build-time** — extracting the `once` binary from Agda (MAlonzo).
   The verified core is **parameterized over an abstract `Interpretation`**, so
   building the compiler needs **no concrete interpretation** — we do not care
   about Linux/seL4/anything here. (This is the moment your intuition names: "we
   build the compiler *before* any program is compiled, and it is
   interpretation-agnostic.")
2. **Once program-compile-time** — the extracted `once` binary compiling a user's
   `.once`. **No Agda in it.** It does **not** know which interpretation will be
   linked; it sees only the declared signatures + effects from the imported
   `.once` interface files, and **cannot check contract proofs**. The asm
   implementation is linked *after*, by the system linker.
3. **Interpretation-verification-time** — *off-line*, in Agda, when someone
   **authors** an interpretation. Each SigOp's contract is discharged here, and
   **discharge is optional in form: proof OR postulate.** A verified target
   (seL4) *can* prove its contracts; an unverified kernel (Linux) *postulates*
   them. Either is a valid discharge — the obligation must *exist*, but it need
   not be *proven*. **All interpretations are equal — none is special, NOT
   Linux.** There is no "built-in interpretation verified when we build Once";
   Linux, seL4, and a user's own are all verified off-line by their authors,
   identically (and this off-line authoring is itself decoupled from #1 and #2).

⟹ The verified core (`elaborate`/`⟦_⟧ˢ`/`⟦_⟧ᴰ`/`faithful`/compile-correctness)
is **parameterized over an ABSTRACT `Interpretation`**, with no concrete
interpretation baked in. `classify-name`/`generic-info`/`generic-semM` were a
hardcoded stand-in for that parameter (and gave Linux special treatment *inside*
the compiler — the bug). This is the **SigOp slice of Plan 0.11's `TrustedBase`
parameterization**.

### The compiler never reads `semM` (2026-06-20)

A `SigOpInfo` bundles `name` + `semM` (machine value semantics) + `effect`
(`EffectShape`). It is worth being precise about *who consumes which field*,
because it clarifies what the `Interpretation` is actually for:

- The **emitter** (the backend that becomes the binary) reads only **`name`**
  (which syscall to emit) — and at most **`effect`** (the optimizer must keep
  effectful ops and may eliminate/reorder `Pure` ones; the backend uses halting
  for control flow). The pure-vs-eff part of `effect` is *already* in the
  arrow's purity `π`.
- **`semM` is never read by the emitter.** It is consumed *only* by `eval` (the
  reference meaning) and the off-line correctness/adequacy proofs (which state
  *what the emitted code must preserve*). In the extracted binary `semM` is a
  dead thunk.

So the `Interpretation` feeds **two different layers** from **one** declared
contract: the **compiler** (via `name` + `effect`) and the **meaning/proof** (via
`semM` + `effect`). Removing `classify-name` fixes the effect (compiler + proof
relevant); removing `generic-semM` fixes the value spec (**meaning-only** — *not*
a compiler need). Both are deleted by the *same* parameter, which is why M0.2
does them together; what `π` cannot supply and the contract must is `semM` and
the **`Halts` vs `Emits`** refinement (does the op terminate the machine).

## Milestone 0 — `0.38-core` (delete the name-guess catch-all)

Parameterize the core over an abstract interpretation and delete the catch-all.

- **M0.1 — `Interpretation` parameter. ✅ DONE** (`Once.SigOp.Interpretation`,
  kernel tier, typechecks). An abstract record providing, per external name, its
  `SigOpInfo` (`semM` + `EffectShape`), with the derived `value-info`/`arrow-info`
  encoding the core's structural rule (a non-arrow / bare-value op is `Pure` —
  effects are deferred onto arrows, D018 — so nothing emits at *build*). The core
  is agnostic to how each instance discharges its contracts (proof/postulate);
  that's off-line (#3 above).
- **M0.2 — Thread it ▶ REMAINING.** Thread the `Interpretation` as a module
  parameter (0.11-style) through the *name-resolution* sites — `Surface.Desugar`,
  `TypeCheck.Elaborate`, `Surface.Elaborate` (build `IR.SigOp`), and
  `Denotation.SourceDenote` / `Adequacy.SourceFaithful` (the meaning + faithful).
  These are the only places a `name` becomes a `SigOpInfo`; downstream reads it
  off the self-describing `IR.SigOp` node, so the parameter need not thread
  further. Delete `generic-info`/`classify-name`/`generic-semM`. Instantiate the
  concrete **Linux** `Interpretation` (from `Syscalls.agda`'s `linux-exit-info` …)
  **at the extraction roots only** (`Compiler`, `SourceFaithful`); the core never
  imports it. Re-extract + Bridge sync + cabal.
- **M0.3 — Discharge `build-pure`. ✅ MOOT.** `build-pure` was *retired* during
  clean-semantics: the `cata`/`ana` cases now reduce via the `cata-body`/`ana-body`
  closure-bridge (`Adequacy.FaithfulLemmas`), which replaced it. `faithful` is
  already total and postulate-free, so there is no `build-pure` postulate to
  discharge. M0.2 must *keep* it postulate-free, not re-establish it.

**Deliverable:** `faithful` total and postulate-free (already true) **and** the
core has no concrete interpretation and no `String → SigOpInfo` catch-all.

### M0.2 FINAL design — the EffectShape is DECLARED in the signature; the compiler is interpretation-blind (2026-06-21)

> **This supersedes every M0.2 design below.** The discussion of 2026-06-21
> established that even an Agda `linux : Interpretation` value with `"linux.exit"`
> in it is the **old hardcoding relocated** — Once must name NO interpretation
> anywhere in `formal/`. The fix is to put the per-arrow `EffectShape` in the
> interpretation's own **signature**, and have the compiler build everything it
> needs by *parsing* that signature. `resolveSigOps`/the partial `Interpretation`
> record (committed) are largely **retired/repurposed** by this — see "fate of
> prior work" below.

**Principles (non-negotiable, from the 2026-06-21 bearings discussion):**
- The compiler **enumerates / names NO interpretation** anywhere in `formal/`.
  It resolves Strata imports like any import, but knows nothing *privileged* —
  Linux is not special; **no** interpretation is. It only ever handles "an
  external arrow with a declared `EffectShape`."
- Interpretations are **user-authorable and linkable**. `Strata/Interpretations/`
  is merely the *default bundle*, not a privileged definition site. Once knows
  about an interpretation **only what that interpretation's own signatures tell
  it** — nothing more.
- An arrow's effect is its **`EffectShape`** (`Pure`/`Emits`/`Halts`), and it is
  **NOT inferable from the type** — a `Unit`-returning eff op could `Halt` or just
  `Emit`-and-continue. So the **signature must declare it**.
- The compiler **never reads `semM`** — only `name` (from the reference) +
  `EffectShape` (from the signature). `semM` is the off-line proof's business
  (the co-located contract), never compiled in.

**Steps:**
1. **Signature grammar declares `EffectShape` via a `!` delimiter (DECIDED 2026-06-21).**
   The TYPE stays a normal Once type; the shape is a separate trailing annotation
   `! <shape>` so types read like every other Once type. Forms:
   - `signature foo : Int -> Unit`          — pure arrow → `Pure` (no annotation)
   - `signature getpid : Eff Unit Int`      — `Eff`, no annotation → `Emits` (default eff)
   - `signature exit : Eff Int Unit ! halts` — `! halts` → `Halts`
   Delimiter is `!` (NOT `@` — that's qualified-name syntax `exit@S`). Shape words:
   `halts`, `emits` (default eff). (Rejected the `Halts A B` arrow-former form: the
   user wants types to look identical to other Once types, so the shape is an
   out-of-type annotation, not a type constructor.)
2. **Parser + the signature/import representation carry the shape** (next to name +
   type — the import context the type-checker already threads).
3. **Elaboration builds the external-ref SigOp from the declared shape**:
   `SigOp (mk-info name <placeholder-semM> <declared-EffectShape>)`, the shape read
   from the imported signature. **DELETE** `classify-name`/`generic-info`/
   `value-info`/`arrow-info` (the name-match) — and **DELETE the hardcoded Agda
   `linux` stub** (`formal/Once/Strata/.../Syscalls.agda`'s `info-linux`); it was
   the relocated hardcode. The compiler builds *nothing* interpretation-specific.
4. **`Strata/Interpretations/Linux/Syscalls.once`**: declare `exit`/`exit0` (and
   any other terminating ops) `halts`. This is the *interpretation* carrying the
   fact — the only place it lives.
5. **Off-line proof**: the meaning `⟦_⟧ˢ` is `∀` an abstract interpretation that
   supplies `semM`; the `EffectShape` comes from the parsed signatures. `faithful`
   stays about that.

**Fate of prior (committed) M0.2 work:**
- The hardcoded Agda `linux` stub — **delete** (it's the relocated hardcode).
- `resolveSigOps` (`Once.SigOp.Resolve`) — **likely retired** for the compiler: the
  shape is built at elaboration from the signature, so there's no late
  "fill-in-the-interpretation" pass. Keep only if the proof wants it; otherwise drop.
- The partial `Interpretation` record (`info : String → Maybe SigOpInfo`) — the
  `EffectShape` half moves to signatures; if anything survives it's a *proof-side*
  `semM` provider, not a compile-time object. Reassess during execution.

**Boundary to keep on the radar:** `EffectShape`'s `Emits`/`Halts` require codomain
`Unit`. Data-returning effectful ops (`getpid : Eff Unit Int`, `fd_read`, …) are
eff *and* non-`Unit`; `EffectShape` can't yet express "effectful, returns a value."
Not compiled at Layer 0, so not blocking `exit` — but a complete model needs
`EffectShape` extended. Tracked, not in scope for the `exit` fix.

### [SUPERSEDED] M0.2 design — LATE resolution as a boundary pass, NOT a parameter cascade (2026-06-20)

**Corrected mechanic.** The plan originally said "thread it as a module parameter
(0.11-style)" — eager resolution, which forces the abstract interpretation through
the entire elaboration pipeline (a ~16-module cascade incl. the type-checker proof
suite). That is the WRONG mechanic. The interpretation is a **SigOp producer**,
exactly like the **arith compiler** — and arith is wired as a single boundary pass
`rewrite-ir : IR → IR × blocks` at `Compile.compileFunWithTarget` (≈ line 388),
with ZERO parameter cascade. An interpretation is the same shape: `name → SigOpInfo`
applied over the IR. So wire it the same way.

**The leaf design.** Resolve LATE, at one seam:
1. **Elaboration emits a generic placeholder** — `IR.SigOp (mk-info name dummy
   Pure)`, carrying the real *name*, a throwaway `semM`/`effect`. INTERPRETATION-
   FREE: desugar / elaborate / the type-checker and their proofs are untouched, and
   the `"linux.exit"` string match leaves the core entirely (the placeholder is
   generic). `value-info`/`arrow-info`/`generic-info`/`classify-name`/`generic-semM`
   are deleted from `Builders`; a single fixed `placeholder-info name` replaces them.
2. **One boundary pass** `resolveSigOps : Interpretation → IR A B → IR A B` — a
   structural IR→IR map (same shape as `Optimize` / arith `rewrite-ir`) replacing
   each `SigOp` node's info with `I.info name`. Wired into `Compile` next to
   `rewrite-ir` (`elaborate → resolveSigOps linux → rewrite-ir → codegen`); the
   placeholder is overwritten before any consumer (optimizer/codegen) reads it.
3. **`eval` / codegen / `⟦_⟧ᴰ` are UNCHANGED** — they read the resolved, self-
   describing node. The self-describing-IR benefit is kept.
4. `I` touches only **two** seams: `resolveSigOps` (the pass) and `⟦_⟧ˢ` (the
   source meaning, which legitimately IS interpretation-relative). `faithful`
   becomes `∀ I → ⟦ resolveSigOps I (elaborate e) ⟧ᴰ ≡ ⟦ e ⟧ˢ I` — the change is
   confined to `Denotation.SourceDenote` + `Adequacy.{FaithfulLemmas,SourceFaithful}`
   (~3 modules), NOT the type-checker.

**Why this is right (not just smaller):** it matches "the compiler never reads
`semM`" — the abstract datum on the node is the *name*; the interpretation-specific
`SigOpInfo` is filled at one seam, exactly as arith fills `arith.block.<digest>`.

**Steps:** (a) `resolveSigOps` + its `eval`/meaning commutation lemma; (b) elaborate
emits `placeholder-info`, delete the `Builders` catch-all; (c) thread `I` through
`⟦_⟧ˢ` + the `faithful` statement; (d) wire `resolveSigOps linux` into `Compile`;
(e) re-extract + Bridge + cabal; verify no `classify-name`/`generic-info`/
`generic-semM`, `faithful` postulate-free, `arith-simple` exits 13.

**NOT core:** concrete interpretation instances (Linux, seL4) and dog-fooding a
user-proven interpretation are off-line, equal, and **deferred** (M1). The
existing `Once/Strata/Interpretations/Linux/Syscalls.agda` is one such *example*
instance — the core must not import it.

## Milestone 1 — external contracts co-located (`per (SigOp × target)`)

Per the design above: `Strata/Interpretations/<plat>/Syscalls.once` declares
signatures; `…/Syscalls/<target>/Contract.agda` discharges `once_<op> ⊨
<op>-contract` for that target. The genuine-external **token** (a SigOpInfo for
an external op is constructible only from such a contract module; internal code
cannot forge it). Closes trust-boundary #2 (impl ⊨ semantics).

**Discharge is proof-OR-postulate, per interpretation × target — NOT "external ⟹
axiom" (2026-06-17).** A SigOp's contract is a producer-discharged *obligation*;
*how* it is discharged is the interpretation's concern and depends on that
platform's verification status:
- **Linux** — the kernel is not formally verified, so its `Contract.agda`
  **postulates** `semM`/`EffectShape`/`impl ⊨ semM` (genuine, irreducible axioms).
- **seL4** (or any verified target) — the contract **can be proven**, connected to
  the kernel's refinement theorems; no postulate needed.
- **Internal** (arith) — proven (already).

The compiler-side mechanism is **discharge-agnostic**: it only requires that *a
contract exists* (the genuine-external token witnesses one), never that it be a
proof or a postulate. So `TrustedBase` shrinks automatically as targets become
verified — the Linux axioms are confined to Linux's contract module, and a seL4
build carries none of them.

## Milestone 2 — fold into 0.11's `TrustedBase`

Once 0.11 lands the `TrustedBase` parameterization, the per-(SigOp × target)
contract postulates become the SigOp slice of that base — confined, per-producer,
no catch-all. This is the convergence point of 0.11 + 0.38.
