# Plan 0.38 — Per-producer SigOp contracts (co-located, per SigOp × target)

> **Future work — design only; implementation deferred.** Drafted while
> diagnosing the Plan 0.36 effectful-cata bug (a SigOp minted for an internal
> function laundered its meaning through the postulated `generic-sem`). This
> plan removes the *class*: a SigOp may only exist with its contract discharged
> by its producer, and the genuine-external contracts live where their subject
> lives — next to the implementation.

## What a SigOp actually is (corrected understanding, 2026-06-09)

A `SigOp` is **just the morphism `A → B`** — nothing more. It is an escape from
**CCC structure**, NOT from soundness. It is defined by Once *because it carries
a contract its producer must follow*. It is used:
- **externally** (syscalls — `linux.exit`, a real `emit`): behavior Once cannot
  derive; the contract is irreducibly **postulated**;
- **internally-but-non-CCC** (the arith compiler's `arith.block.<digest>`):
  Once-generated subroutines that leave CCC; the contract is **proven** (the
  arith compiler discharges emit-correspondence + `PreservesCCC`).

So the soundness unit is: **a SigOp is sound iff its producer discharged its
contract.** Internal producers prove it; external producers postulate it. The
Plan 0.36 bug was a producer (an extraction in the cata path) minting a SigOp
**without discharging the contract** — permitted only because the contract is
decoupled from construction and a generic catch-all supplies it.

## Two trust boundaries, one of them currently invisible

For an external SigOp like `linux.exit`:
1. **Agda-level contract** — `PreservesCCC` + semI/semM coherence. Today:
   postulated/discharged somewhere downstream (per-name, partly via the generic
   pure path).
2. **Implementation ⊨ semantics** — does the linked `once_exit` asm actually do
   what `semM` says? **Today this is nowhere** — `Syscalls.x86_64` is linked at
   the end; nothing in the proof connects it to `semM`. Not even a postulate —
   an unstated assumption outside the verified boundary.

Boundary #2 is the real gap: the trust sits *away from its subject* (the asm),
so it can't be audited.

## The design

**Co-locate the contract with the implementation, at (SigOp × target)
granularity.**

- The `.once` interface file (`Syscalls.once`) declares only signatures.
- Each target implementation (`Syscalls.x86_64`, `Syscalls.riscv64`, …) is the
  asm that must satisfy the contract.
- Therefore each (interface-file × target) gets a paired **Agda contract
  module** holding the per-SigOp contract postulates for that file's ops on that
  target — e.g. `Strata/Interpretations/Linux/Syscalls/X86-64/Contract.agda`
  postulating `once_exit ⊨ linux.exit-contract`, `once_write ⊨ …`, etc.
  (Granularity of the *obligation* is per SigOp × target — honest, since the
  trust is per-asm; the *module* is per interface-file × target, mirroring the
  `Syscalls.once` ↔ `Syscalls.<target>` pairing.)
- These postulates ARE the external trusted base — genuine axioms (you cannot
  prove a syscall). They feed Plan 0.11's `TrustedBase` as the *confined,
  per-producer* external axioms.

**Internal producers prove, never postulate.** Arith already does; a cata that
ever genuinely emits a SigOp (rare — its algebra is normally CCC code) would
have to prove its contract too. No internal producer touches the external
contract modules.

**Eliminate the generic catch-all.** `generic-info : String → SigOpInfo A B`
(materialising postulated `generic-sem` for *any* name) is the laundering
vector — it lets a producer mint a SigOp for a name with no real contract. A
SigOpInfo should only be constructible **with** its contract (from the
producer's contract module / a proof), so a contractless SigOp is a type error
(this is Plan 0.11's "obligation local to construction"). The opaque external
semantics survive only behind a **genuine-external token** that a Strata
signature + its contract module can mint and internal code cannot forge.

## Relationship to 0.11

0.11 parameterizes the trusted base and bans `postulate` outside `TrustedBase`.
0.38 says *what the SigOp entries of that base are*: not one `generic-sem`
catch-all, but a set of per-(SigOp × target) implementation-contract axioms,
co-located with their impls and surfaced into `TrustedBase`. 0.11 makes them
visible; 0.38 makes them honest (per-asm) and unforgeable (no catch-all).

## Deferred

Everything. This is the design record. Sequencing: land 0.11's
parameterization first (so there's a `TrustedBase` to put these in), then 0.38
restructures the SigOp axioms into per-producer contract modules and removes
`generic-info`'s catch-all. Anchors: [[project_effectful_cata_blocker]] (the bug
that motivated this), [[project_plan_0_25_landed]] (EffectShape contract),
[[project_plan_0_26_landed]] (Pure-SigOp discharge), [[feedback_arith_no_value_spec]].
